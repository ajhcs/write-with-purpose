Six attachments were inspected as supplied. Five are retained as distinct candidate exemplars; **How Will You Measure Your Life? is the 2012 book**, not the same-named article, and is excluded under the accepted initial scope. A 786-page *Review of Particle Physics* is a collaborative reference compilation, with separately authored topical reviews and listings, not a single original-theory paper. The record in [nonmedical-uploads.json](nonmedical-uploads.json) gives source hashes, proposed forms, outlines, boundaries, 14 anchored samples and alternative readings. Annotations are editorial proposals, not verified function labels or evidence of quality. Samples are selective, including first, middle and late source regions; no full reading of either long volume is claimed.

The 1998 particle review's first-page DOI is `10.1007/s100529800944`, which differs from its filename. Its title-page abstract and introduction give different numbers of new measurements, and its introduction describes an earlier update window; this looks like source-internal carryover, not a discrepancy to silently reconcile. It contains 786 PDF pages while the journal cover gives pp. 1–794. Local page mapping, formula extraction and completeness must be checked before measurements. The supercollider article combines a review of motivations and signatures with its own quantitative calculations; its classification is a review/analysis hybrid. The IEEE cloud paper is a research-challenges overview, not a formal systematic review. The HBR copies identify original years 1998 and 1999 and reprint years 2004 and 2005, respectively.

## Feature observations and implications for measurement

- **F01 — Length:** File page totals are not body word counts: 129 pages for the collider review; 786 for the particle compilation; 7 for cloud; 12 and 13 for the HBR packages; 168 for the excluded book. Title, abstract, contents, figures, reference lists, summaries, advertisements and other back matter require separate zoning. No word-length estimate or target is justified here.
- **F02 — Structure:** The collider paper moves from introduction and preliminaries through multiple physics topics to conclusions and an appendix. The particle volume combines introduction, summary tables, topical reviews and particle listings; its reviews have their own local structures. Cloud has an introduction, overview, paradigm comparison, adoption challenges, interoperability and conclusion. The two HBR essays organize their arguments by leadership components or self-assessment questions. These are distinct patterns, not mandatory section orders.
- **F03 — Proportions:** No section shares have been measured. The compilation's embedded entries and the collider paper's figures/equations make naive page or extracted-token ratios especially unreliable. HBR editorial summaries and pull quotes require separation from article prose.
- **F04 — Paragraph shape:** The samples range from concise contrast to extended background and applied examples. PDF extraction introduces line breaks, hyphenation, merged columns and repeated pull quotes; whitespace counts in JSON describe selected spans only, not validated paragraph distributions.
- **F05 — Sentence shape:** The articles mix statements, questions and examples, but no sentence counts or distributions are asserted. Physics notation and extraction defects would need specialized segmentation.
- **F06 — Word length:** Physics names, formula fragments, abbreviations and cloud service terminology would distort simple character counts; no word-length comparison is claimed.
- **F07 — Evidence and examples:** The collider review combines citations, equations, calculated rates and figures; the particle compilation records measurements, tables, citations and evaluation procedures; the cloud paper describes and compares cited technical approaches. The HBR authors employ research claims, reported cases, hypothetical illustrations and practical arguments in different mixtures. The examples' use is observed; their truth and causal effectiveness have not been evaluated.
- **F08 — Presentation:** Equations, figures, tables, reference listings and sidebars differ substantially from continuous prose. The particle volume is also a navigable reference. The cloud article uses nested technical subsections; the HBR packages contain editorial summaries and promotional back matter. These visible features must be inventoried separately from evidentiary roles.
- **F09 — Communicative functions:** The annotations show orientation, definition, report, assertion, support, explanation, comparison, illustration, qualification and narration, sometimes in the same span. The book prologue's reunion narrative is not interchangeable with the collider review's technical scope statement. Labels have plausible alternatives recorded in JSON; no independent second coder has checked them.

## Blank contribution patterns for testing

These are source-grounded functional prompts, not sentence stems, author voice, or required paragraph recipes. Each arrow may connect spans across multiple paragraphs or combine within one; use only when it helps the current author and reader.

- **Technical review/analysis:** establish a theoretical or practical frontier → specify an analytical question and scope → describe assumptions and calculations or existing knowledge → interpret implications → qualify sensitivity to assumptions. Alternative: begin with a concrete process or result → connect it to the broader frontier → compare cases → explain implications and limits. Grounded in the collider paper's opening, heavy-flavor case and approximation discussion.
- **Technical challenges perspective:** describe the setting and open questions → distinguish related concepts → compare approaches and their conditions → identify an unresolved issue. Alternative: start from a concrete interoperability problem → describe approaches at different levels → compare what each addresses → identify residual gaps. Grounded in the cloud article's introduction and intermediary-layer discussion.
- **Practitioner argument:** introduce a situation and position → define a proposed capability or choice → use a reported or hypothetical case to illustrate it → explain its proposed significance → state a condition or limitation. Alternative: pose a self-assessment question → contrast relevant situations → offer a recommendation and reasons. The Goleman and Drucker articles do not warrant a shared fixed length or a fourteen-paragraph norm.
- **Reference compilation:** state the scope and types of material → explain selection/evaluation practices → organize entries by a navigable classification → present data, source references and local interpretation where needed. Individual topical reviews may require different sequences. This is a container-level pattern, not a prose article template.
- **Reflective narrative (excluded book, contextual only):** recount a personal situation → introduce a tension or question → connect a concrete case to a concept → reflect on its possible implications. Another approach begins with the concept and applies it to an example. Book-derived observations do not enter the initial article corpus.

These are observed conventions in a small, user-provided, heterogeneous set, not requirements, population ranges, evidence of writing quality, or instructions to imitate an author's voice. The personal and professional claims in the sources are attributed to their authors and have not been independently validated.

## Reproducibility and boundaries

The JSON records source SHA-256 hashes and exact zero-based, end-exclusive Unicode code-point spans against private UTF-8 PyMuPDF page-text files. It also records each extracted-page and span hash and whitespace word count. The private cache path is `/workspace/repos/builderbob/.writing-corpus-cache/analysis/review-nonmedical/`; full source passages remain there. Re-extraction by another engine or text ordering will change offsets. PDF page numbers are one-based, not necessarily the printed page numbers. Two-column ordering, figure/equation extraction and OCR quality remain manual-review risks; selected span counts are not section metrics.
