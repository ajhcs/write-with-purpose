# HTML analysis methods and coverage

`python3 experiments/corpus-analysis-2026-11-16/analysis/analyze_html.py` reads existing manifests and cached source files only. It does not retrieve network content. It uses the common `metrics.py` engine; word and sentence rules are described in the parent method note. `html-records.jsonl` contains source metadata, hashes, statuses, extraction diagnostics and derived measurements. Normalized plaintext blocks are stored outside this repository under `/workspace/repos/builderbob/.writing-corpus-cache/analysis/html/`. `body_sha256` hashes the exact compact UTF-8 JSON block array before its trailing file newline. `source_sha256` hashes the raw capture. Each section ID identifies an individual heading occurrence, not a normalized section title. Source HTML is never copied into this repository.

In the personal manifest 129 records are body candidates: 75 measured, 43 policy-held, 10 failed extraction and one PDF assigned to the PDF workflow. The remaining 31 saved HTML captures are noncandidates: 25 excluded and six policy-held. Thus all 49 policy-flagged saved HTML files are held before HTML parsing or analytical processing; only file bytes, size and hash are read. The 156 no-file outcomes represent retrieval outcomes, not claims that files disappeared. A hash mismatch or duplicate raw content, if encountered on rerun, receives an explicit status; duplicate content is not measured twice in this HTML pass. Cross-format and cross-collection work-level deduplication is separate.

For the archive, the prior offline Verdana-block and double-break extraction is reproduced and sent through the common metric engine. This is one author's archive, not a generic essay norm. Four previously manually screened works retain their provisional personal-reflective or argument-perspective labels; all other works, including the personal retrieval candidates, have `FORM_UNRESOLVED`. The 225 entries include four whose extraction falls below the 100-prose-word triage threshold. The records include hashes, dates, and archive-selection provenance where available.

For other HTML, a standard-library DOM tree selects an `article`, `main`, or recognized body container with the most paragraph text. The expected title must overlap a page title, H1, or Open Graph title by at least 60% of expected title tokens. This is a triage heuristic, not identity verification; no match or fewer than 100 prose words is an extraction failure. Navigation, sidebars, author biographies, related content, newsletter elements, controls and figures are suppressed when identifiable through element type, role or attributes. Headers indicating related content are omitted, but page-specific markup can defeat these rules. Paragraphs, headings, list items, code and quotations remain distinct; figure and table content is not prose. Object and link counts are incidental traversal counts, not complete citations or object inventories. No citation function is inferred. Every result is provisional, and body completeness, edition and author identity remain unverified unless explicitly reported by the original audit. Title match does not establish article body completeness. Embedded inline markup, footnotes, recommendations or partial paywalls may still affect measurements; individual boundaries need review.

The FRUS extractor targets the named document div and uses the earlier transcription extractor, excluding editorial footnotes, the opener, and subject routing, while retaining decision choices as list items. The six represent selected historical foreign-policy decisions and do not establish general business memo norms. The Heller source is an official amicus brief, not a judicial opinion or a representative legal corpus. Its source title matches the page; the underlying prior manifest left identity and completeness unresolved.

Two manual boundary spot checks were performed against the cached DOM and normalized block endpoints. In `LF-f2db6fcab11c`, the selected main content contained the target essay alongside a publisher introduction, a control and a numbered endnote; these specific items were removed. In `official-heller-brief`, the target brief was followed by signatures and numbered notes; extraction now stops before the signature. `BAE-13fadc84b9cf` was also checked at both endpoints and contained article paragraphs within the marked article-body container, with no visible adjacent recommendations at those endpoints. These checks do not certify other records or completeness. Source-specific corrections are encoded in the reproducible script and reported per record.

The Heller HTML encodes much of its legal structure as ordinary paragraphs, so front matter and paragraph-styled headings may enter prose counts. The FRUS extractor's prior handling of routing and decision lines differs from the generic HTML path. These source-specific conventions limit direct comparisons; heading and list counts are representation-dependent.

The earlier Longform publisher probe recorded 25 outcomes; 11 have saved captures, whose paths are derived exactly from the script's SHA-256-of-source-URL naming convention. Six were provisionally identified by the prior extractor as article bodies; all six are held before parsing because the original robots rules and AI-input signals were not retained. One, On Self-Respect, is byte-identical to the held personal record `LF-686cac71e9fa` and the duplicate is explicitly linked. The remaining five captures are excluded based on the earlier extractor's `no_article_body` outcome; that earlier outcome was itself unreviewed and no fresh body inspection was performed. The other 14 probe outcomes did not result in a saved capture and remain recorded in the original probe manifest. The Gettysburg probe also has a documented 600-second crawl-delay error from the original acquisition, as recorded in the prior essay summary. These capture-level statuses do not imply that an unavailable or excluded work lacks a full text elsewhere.
