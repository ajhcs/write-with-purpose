# Round 1 position: editing and substantive improvement

An improvement request authorizes an editor to **improve the thought, organization and expression**, not merely remove defects. The current skills already permit much of that work, including substantial rewrites when requested; nevertheless, their “smallest set of changes” and defect-first stopping language can make a general request to improve a draft default to surface repair. I propose choosing the depth the task authorizes, testing a meaningful alternative when warranted, and minimizing unrelated changes—not minimizing the overall diff.

Copy editing, thought development, argument restructuring and expression improvement are distinct, overlapping activities. “Fix punctuation” authorizes the first; “strengthen this argument” can authorize the middle two; “make the prose more vivid” can authorize the fourth. A user's explicit scope controls. Preserving an idea is a semantic obligation, not a requirement to preserve every sentence or its order. Conversely, more confident or forceful prose must not invent evidence, cause, experience or certainty.

## Five mechanisms

**EI-01 — Choose authorized depth before minimizing changes.** On an open-ended improvement request, identify the reader's task and actively consider a consequential revision, including structure, before choosing the candidate. For correction-only work, keep edits local. Example: “The pilot was delayed. The vendor had no test environment. We should defer the launch until integration is tested” can become “Defer the launch until integration is tested. The pilot was delayed, and the vendor had no test environment.” The decision and condition now appear first; the revision preserves both facts without inventing a causal link. It would be inappropriate where the author requested punctuation only or a reflective essay needs a delayed conclusion.

**EI-02 — Develop a bounded inference bridge.** When an authorized development task leaves the relationship between observation and intended conclusion implicit, write the missing connection from supplied premises and challenge plausible alternative readings. Given a contractor referral, three status updates, a still-leaking window and the author's distinction between status and repair: “The request had been sent to a contractor and residents received three status updates, but the window was still leaking. An update can show activity without showing that the repair is complete.” This develops the thought with a qualified inference; it does not claim the contractor caused a delay. When a bridge would resolve a deliberately open scene or requires an unknown premise, retain the openness or mark the question.

**EI-03 — Recompose around the reader's decision.** Map the claim, reasons, counterconsiderations and decision condition as needed, without imposing paragraph labels; compare a different grouping or order. Given “Option A can start now but uses our remaining budget. Option B starts next month and costs less. We should choose B unless the launch must happen this month,” the revision can lead: “Choose Option B unless the launch must happen this month. B starts next month and costs less; A can start now but would use the remaining budget.” The reader can recover the decision and tradeoff together. Required ordering and an author's purposeful narrative reveal take precedence; recommendation-first is not a universal template.

**EI-04 — Improve expression by preserving its job.** Test whether different phrasing makes the intended effect more vivid or connected, while retaining the author's stance and supplied facts. “The status page said complete. The window still leaked whenever it rained” can become “The status page said the job was complete, but rain still came through the window.” The contrast is more connected. Keep the original if clipped rhythm is the author's deliberate effect; a new synonym alone is not an improvement.

**EI-05 — Stop after a useful comparison.** For requested substantive improvement, compare the original with the strongest authorized candidate against what the reader should understand, feel or do, as well as any loss of voice or substance. Choose the original if no alternative earns its tradeoff. For example, “We should wait. The trial missed its deadline, and the supplier has not supplied the safety report” can become “We should wait: the trial missed its deadline, and the supplier has not supplied the safety report.” A more specific revision—“Wait for the supplier's safety report before proceeding”—would introduce a dependency that the supplied text does not establish and must be rejected unless separately supported. This is a bounded comparison, not an automatic rewriting loop or mandatory report to the user.

In each mechanism, the **model** makes the contextual editorial judgment; **code** can preserve snapshots, show a diff and check declared limits or literal protected terms; the **author** sets the task and constraints and judges acceptance. A diff, lexical count or model-assigned voice score cannot certify fidelity. Ordinary reversible edits do not require a permission interruption.

## Exact baseline changes proposed

- Replace the edit skill's “smallest set of changes that resolves the requested problem” with: “Choose the depth authorized by the request. For correction-only work make the necessary local corrections; for requested improvement actively test a change in development, organization or expression when it could materially serve the reader. Preserve effective language and explicit constraints, and avoid unrelated changes.”
- Expand its defect-first instruction to: “Identify the reader task and either a defect to repair or a supported opportunity to improve the thought or its expression. For an improvement request compare a consequential candidate with the original; stop when no further authorized change offers a defensible benefit.”
- Extend the writing skill's reader edit: “Also consider developing a supported inference, recomposing the argument, or improving its expression when the task invites it; select on reader and author benefit, not word count or minimal diff.”

These are candidates for debate, not changes to installed skills. EI-02 and EI-03 develop existing guidance; their novelty is the explicit comparative operation and resolution of under-editing, relative to the baselines inspected, not a claim of a unique invention.

## Evidence and limits

[P16 Yeung](../../evidence-integration-2026-11-16/reviews/genre-moves.md) describes 22 historical authentic business reports and a finding–interpretation–recommendation relationship whose order can vary (PDF p. 14). [P17 Cotos and colleagues](../../evidence-integration-2026-11-16/reviews/genre-moves.md) describe disciplinary Methods moves, overlapping functions and non-prescribed order (pp. 6, 8–9); these studies inform possible choices, not effects of our edits or mandatory form sequences. [P10 and P11](../../evidence-integration-2026-11-16/reviews/funding-persuasion.md) are observational grant studies: their language and funding associations cannot establish that adding adjectives, certainty or complexity causes better writing or awards. They challenge blanket simplification or anti-emphasis rules, while source fidelity continues to govern each claim.

The local calibration found original exact-set agreement of 21/36 versus 18/36 for the proposed revision, with no semantic gold; it did not demonstrate improved function coding. Local genre samples show structural variety but are small and purposive. Neither is a test of these editing mechanisms. Compare each mechanism with the current skills on fresh authorized tasks, separating independent reader task performance, author recognition of voice and ideas, factual fidelity, repair burden and elapsed work. Include correction-only briefs and drafts where keeping the original is best; retain failures. No claim of proven reader gain or product advantage follows yet.
