# Worked operations and counterexamples

These use original synthetic development cases supplied for this debate. They demonstrate the candidate's intended operation; they are not held-out tests, observed reader gains, author acceptance or gold annotations. The current skills could reach the same outcomes. The proposed difference is a more explicit decision procedure, not evidence that the baseline necessarily fails.

## Beyond local correction: reconsider the recommendation (E02)

**Request:** improve reasoning and recommendation, including structure; reconsider the staffing level if unsupported; retain the goal of reducing backlog.

**Original:** “Our backlog increased from 12 to 30 tickets last month. The team closed 18 tickets. We should hire two analysts because the backlog is now 30.”

The supplied record adds 36 arrivals and says ticket mix, seasonality and the capacity another analyst would add are unknown. No hiring or budget decision has been made.

**Candidate:**

> Assess whether additional staffing is needed to reduce the backlog before deciding on two hires. Last month, 36 tickets arrived and 18 were closed, raising the backlog from 12 to 30. Those totals do not establish the staffing level needed: the ticket mix, seasonality and the capacity another analyst would add are unknown.

**Operation:** R01 treats the recommendation itself as revisable because the request explicitly authorizes that work. R02 separates the observed flow imbalance from the missing inference to two hires. R03 moves the decision now warranted to the opening and develops its rationale in prose. The source arithmetic is consistent: 12 + 36 − 18 = 30. That mechanical fact does not estimate staffing capacity or establish the cause of the backlog.

**What improves, provisionally:** the paragraph now makes a decision under uncertainty instead of presenting a specific headcount as a consequence of backlog size. It develops the reasoning and reorganizes the argument rather than simply adding a hedge to “hire two analysts.”

**Tradeoff:** it does not provide an immediate hiring number and is longer. That is warranted by this request and these missing premises, but could be unhelpful if the author supplied a separate valid staffing model. In that case, use the model and its assumptions; do not substitute this assessment request reflexively.

A limited candidate that only changes “should hire” to “may need to hire” still leaves the headcount ungrounded. That illustrates an insufficient local repair; it is not a measured baseline output. The old skills already flag missing premises and could produce the same substantive revision. Testing whether the new trigger makes that behavior more reliable remains future work.

## Keep the original after considering an expressive alternative (E03)

The request protects the repetition, fixed story details and unresolved ending:

> I kept the ticket. It proved nothing, and I knew that. Still, I kept it. By morning the address had smudged; the date stayed. I do not know why that mattered.

A feasible alternative is:

> I kept the ticket. I knew it proved nothing. Still, I kept it. By morning, the address had smudged, but the date stayed. I do not know why that mattered.

The second version makes the narrator's knowledge direct and changes the temporal sentence's cadence. It does not clearly improve the requested flow; the original's “and I knew that” allows the admission to follow the observation, and the semicolon leaves the two surviving details side by side. These are editorial readings, not objective aesthetic scores. Given the brief's protected choices and no clear benefit from the alternative, retain the original. Do not invent a lesson explaining why the date matters. R01 permits improvement without demanding it; R03 permits an unclear effect and an unchanged result.
