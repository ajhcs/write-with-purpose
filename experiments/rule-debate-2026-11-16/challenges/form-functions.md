# Round 2: challenges from form and function

## FF-C01 → CE-04

An issue-based stop can end an authorized improvement after fixing only the first conspicuous problem: 'no named unresolved issue' depends on whether the editor ever inspected the broader structure and reader objective. At the same time an open-ended search for a superior arrangement would waste time and invite unwanted rewrites. Evaluation of annotation schemes is a distinct research activity, not a mandatory runtime step.

**Case.** A user asks to improve a decision memo. The editor fixes a vague sentence and checks the final artifact, but never notices that the only recommendation and its limiting budget condition are buried on separate pages. No issue remains on its local list, yet the request for meaningful improvement has not been examined against the document's purpose. In contrast, the same reorganization would exceed a punctuation-only request.

**Proposed wording.** For substantive improvement, before stopping, perform one task-scaled whole-passage reader review: can the intended reader recover the contribution, consequential reasoning, and conditions, and is there a material structural opportunity supported by the supplied text? Address any consequential issue within scope, or retain the current organization when it serves the task. Stop once that review and relevant checks are complete; do not run additional passes or generate alternatives without a specific expected benefit. If the requested deliverable is a research annotation, follow its independently frozen sampling, unit, adjudication and agreement protocol regardless of whether a label would alter an edit.

**Novelty and concession.** CE-04's separation of label reliability, reader outcomes and ordinary editing is stronger than my FF-03 if FF-03 is read as an annotation research rule. Current structure-and-purpose already asks for a standalone reader edit and permits movement; the candidate contribution is an explicit stop condition tied to a task-scaled structural review, not discovery of reader editing or authorization to rewrite.

## FF-C02 → RL03

The comparison is conditioned on a concrete failure, which is already a useful restriction, but requiring two fully materialized edits whenever a local repair seems insufficient may inflate work and obscure that a user may want editing without a substantial rewrite. A realistic comparison can be brief, internal and asymmetric: a local repair can be rejected by its demonstrated inability to fix the issue, without drafting two complete alternatives. This also applies to my FF-04.

**Case.** A three-page draft repeats a necessary explanation at two independent entry points; its other paragraphs are adequate. A requested cleanup can remove a redundant phrase while retaining both explanations. Producing an entire coordinated rewrite to compare would consume effort and potentially destroy the entry-point function. Conversely, an improvement request for a memo with separated decision and qualification may justify moving both, even if the source change is extensive.

**Proposed wording.** For an authorized improvement, diagnose a concrete reader failure. When local and structural interventions are both plausible, compare their expected gain and specific loss at the least costly level that makes the choice reviewable; materialize a second complete candidate only if the tradeoff cannot otherwise be judged or the author requests options. Execute the better-supported permitted repair, including a coordinated rewrite when needed; correction-only, requested format, protected language, and intentional voice remain controlling. A request to edit is not automatically a request to restructure, while a request for substantive improvement may authorize it.

**Novelty and concession.** The existing edit skill permits coordinated changes, a substantial rewrite when requested and an explicit organizational change; the writing reference already recommends move/delete comparisons. RL03 is not novel because rewrites were previously forbidden. Its potentially novel contribution is a deliberate reader-outcome comparison across intervention scales, subject to a burden test. FF-04's phrasing should be narrowed in the same way.

## Concessions

- CE-01's explicit same-reader-task before/after exercise is more falsifiable than FF-01's structural-choice presentation alone; use it when a material change needs evaluation, without claiming the model simulates real readers.
- CE-03's concrete gain and strongest loss, including retaining the original, sharpens FF-01 and FF-04: preserving deliberate delay or a repeated prerequisite can beat an apparently cleaner reordering.
- RL03 already limits structural comparison to authorized substantial improvement and a concrete failure; my objection refines execution cost rather than claiming it requires alternatives for routine copy edits.
- My FF-04 overstated the contrast with the current skill: its smallest set is already qualified by resolving the requested problem, and the skill explicitly allows coordinated changes and substantial rewrites. A credible change must specify when to examine a broader intervention and what benefit justifies it, not portray the existing rule as a ban.
- FF-03's consequence-based runtime shortcut must not weaken a standalone corpus study: independent annotations, explicit unresolved/missing states and predeclared evaluation units remain part of that separate deliverable.
