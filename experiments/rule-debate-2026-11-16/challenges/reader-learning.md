# Round 2 challenges: reader knowledge and learning

## RL-CH01 → FF-02: A fork can manufacture the author's options

The proposal's two “viable warrants” can be a false choice. “Absences rose after the schedule changed; we should hire staff” establishes neither that staffing is the bottleneck nor that the new shifts caused absences. Other explanations and reasons for hiring remain possible. Presenting two attractive forks can lead the author to adopt a model-generated rationale they did not hold, even when both forks are called hypothetical. It can also interrupt a correction-only task that does not require resolving the reasoning.

**Proposed replacement:** “First find the author's stated reason in supplied material. If it is missing, preserve the observation and recommendation as separate claims; ask what connects them only if the gap prevents the requested decision. Offer alternative warrants only when the author asks for reasoning help or supplied evidence bounds the alternatives; label them as model hypotheses and include neither/other. Do not silently select one or call it viable without evidence.”

Generating competing warrants and testing their consequences is an operational addition to simply asking for support. Alternative structures, inference provenance, and author control already appear in the existing Astra strategy, so the novelty is the bounded fork and discriminating evidence—not the general principle of seeking reasons.

## RL-CH02 → VN-05: An explanation is not a request for homework

The proposal avoids lessons for urgent or prose-only work, but “offer an optional transfer exercise” can still interrupt delivery or burden an author who requested only the rationale for a single edit. My own RL04 has the same weakness. For example, someone preparing an urgent scientific note asks, “Why did you move that condition?” One sentence explaining which claim it limits is responsive; offering another passage to solve introduces a new task.

**Proposed replacement for VN-05 and RL04:** “If asked, explain the consequential edit briefly against the actual wording and reader consequence. Offer an exercise only when the author requests instruction, practice, or a previously established learning workflow; defer it until the writing deliverable is complete. If practice is requested, let the author attempt first and permit immediate decline. Never infer learning from acceptance.”

Examples, feedback, brief rationales and later transfer already exist in tutoring precedents and the prior Astra strategy. Deferring an explicitly wanted exercise and separating rationale from practice is a useful workflow refinement, not a new teaching theory.

## RL-CH03 → FF-04: Compare candidates when the comparison changes the decision

The current editing skill already permits coordinated edits and substantial reorganization when requested; “smallest set of changes that resolves the requested problem” is not a categorical ban on rewriting. My RL03 should make the same correction. Requiring a local and structural version whenever both are plausible risks generating unnecessary drafts: when the only material problem is a scattered recommendation and its condition, moving them together may already be the obvious supported fix. Conversely, forcing the recommendation to the front could damage an intentionally staged narrative.

**Proposed replacement for FF-04 and RL03:** “For an authorized improvement, choose the intervention that resolves a specific problem while honoring purpose and constraints. When more than one materially different repair is plausible and consequential, compare their reader effects; otherwise implement the supported repair directly.” Test insufficient local repairs and unnecessary rewrites as separate harms. Authorized substantive rewriting is baseline behavior; selective comparison of concrete alternatives is the unvalidated operational hypothesis.

## Concessions

FF-03 is stronger than my RL01 at determining whether an ambiguous interpretation must be resolved: if competing readings would lead to the same justified edit, preserve the ambiguity and distinguish unassessed from unresolved. RL01 adds a localized reader-task probe and source lookup, but should not demand that every ambiguity be labeled.

VN-03 is stronger than my RL02 for unusual expressive language: identify what the imagery or rhythm contributes, then compare a repair that retains it against a plain alternative only when the task permits. The existing skills already reject uniform simplicity and protect voice; contextual comparison is the testable mechanism, not a claim of having invented that principle.
