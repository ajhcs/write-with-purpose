# Primary ranking and document sources

Accessed 16 November 2026. No full-text measurement or legal advice is supplied here.

- [HeinOnline Scholarly Impact Rankings: Most-Cited Articles](https://access.heinonline.com/HOL/scholarly-impact-ranking/comprehensive-articles) lists article ranks, citation counts in time windows, cumulative citations, and a separate past-12-month access column. Its provider-specific 2026-11-16 web snapshot identifies *The Right to Privacy* as rank 1 (5,936 cumulative citations), *The Path of the Law* as rank 2 (5,007), and *The Problem of Social Cost* as rank 3 (4,698). Counts and ranks can change. The table contains entries outside the six journals in this discovery set, as well as forewords and other genres; inspect item type. The direct local HTTP request received 403, but the public rendered table was available through web search. No automated extraction or equivalence to OpenAlex counts is asserted.
- [Shapiro and Pearse, *The Most-Cited Law Review Articles of All Time*](https://repository.law.umich.edu/mlr/vol110/iss8/2/), 110 Michigan Law Review 1483 (2012), explicitly describes all-time and twenty-year top-100 lists. This is a historical study and its rankings are a 2012 snapshot, not a present-day count or reading measure. Consult the paper and its methods directly before reusing its ranked entries.
- [OpenAlex work metadata and citation definition](https://help.openalex.org/data/works/attributes/) and [search/sort guidance](https://help.openalex.org/how-to/searching/): the supplied leads use OpenAlex citation counts, not HeinOnline counts. Counts reflect successful reference matches in the OpenAlex graph and are affected by coverage, matching and duplicate/merged records. Six source-specific searches, not a comprehensive legal bibliography or cross-provider impact ranking, were performed.
- [U.S. Supreme Court opinions](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/opinions.aspx) and [U.S. Reports](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/USReports.aspx) offer authoritative judicial-opinion sources. The Court explains that principal, concurring, dissenting, per curiam, and in-chambers opinions are distinct. The Reporter's syllabus is a separate unit from the opinion. A bound volume contains many cases and other front/back matter; do not treat the volume as one opinion.
- [Department of Justice Solicitor General brief collection](https://www.justice.gov/osg/supreme-court-briefs) identifies docket, filing date, and brief type (merits, amicus, petition response, etc.). A brief advocates for a party in a particular procedural posture; it is not a judicial opinion or law-review article.
- [DOJ Office of Legal Counsel opinions by volume](https://www.justice.gov/olc/opinions-volume) and [written-opinion guidance](https://www.justice.gov/olc/best-practices-olc-legal-advice-and-written-opinions) provide historical executive legal advice and records. Identify the author, recipient, question, date, provenance and present status separately from a decision memo seeking approval. Some opinions can be withdrawn or superseded; DOJ's [2009 release](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/department-justice-releases-nine-office-legal-counsel-memoranda-and-opinions) illustrates this. Avoid using historical positions as current law.

These sources describe distinct audiences, authority and legal tasks. For writing research, classify and analyze law-review articles, student notes/comments, judicial opinions, advocacy briefs, executive opinions, and internal action memoranda separately. Retain legal citations, quotations, footnotes, procedural posture, issue, rule, analysis and disposition as separate structural/evidentiary observations where appropriate; do not require IMRAD. Citation and downloads are discovery signals, not legal correctness or editorial quality.

## Individual pre-2015 examples for separate screening

- **Judicial opinion:** [*Brown v. Board of Education*, 347 U.S. 483 (1954)](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/usrep/usrep347/usrep347483/usrep347483.pdf), official U.S. Reports reproduction. Separate the Court's opinion from the syllabus; do not treat later legal developments as part of this historical text.
- **Amicus merits brief:** [United States brief in *District of Columbia v. Heller*](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/district-columbia-v-heller-amicus-merits), January 2008, filed by the Solicitor General. This is advocacy in a specified procedural role, and its presence in a notable case is a selection signal rather than a separate writing-quality judgment.
- **Executive operational memorandum:** [Deputy Attorney General memorandum, *District of Columbia v. Heller*](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/dag/legacy/2008/11/10/dag-memo-07032008.pdf), July 3, 2008. Its purpose and audience differ from an OLC legal opinion or a request for an executive decision; inspect the whole memorandum before assigning its more specific function.
- **Executive legal-writing guidance:** [OLC memorandum, *Best Practices for OLC Legal Advice and Written Opinions*](https://www.justice.gov/sites/default/files/olc/legacy/2010/08/26/olc-legal-advice-opinions.pdf), July 16, 2010. This is guidance on a form, not itself an example of the legal opinion under review. Later guidance or changes in law may supersede historical practice.
