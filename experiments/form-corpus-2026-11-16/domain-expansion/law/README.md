# Legal writing: historical citation discovery

The collection contains **390 distinct OpenAlex metadata leads** selected from six US law reviews, 65 per venue, with OpenAlex publication year through 2014. These are discoverable bibliographic records, **not 390 verified law-review articles, original scholarship, full texts, suitable exemplars, or legal authorities**. They preserve provider query rank, citation count, author/title/year/DOI or landing page, provider classification, and query timestamp. `records.csv` and `records.jsonl` are equivalent. `query-receipts.json` preserves exact provider requests, result counts, filter choices, exclusions, source IDs, and source-response hashes. Reproduce with `python3 experiments/form-corpus-2026-11-16/domain-expansion/law/collect.py`, which uses cached metadata responses stored outside the repo in `/workspace/tmp/builderbob/writing-corpus/legal-openalex/`.

The queries limit primary source to Harvard Law Review, Yale Law Journal, Michigan Law Review, Stanford Law Review, Columbia Law Review, or University of Chicago Law Review; ask OpenAlex for `type:article` dated through 2014; sort that venue's results by `cited_by_count:desc`; and keep the first 65 distinct titled records with known year and no provider retraction flag. The sample favors prestigious U.S. journals and misses other legal systems, sources and languages. Only one page of 100 per venue was screened. Two records lack author metadata, and three lack DOI, though all have a DOI or landing-page link. Source records, title/year deduplication and provider retraction flags can be incomplete. Counts and classification must be checked before selection. The six source-specific ranks do not combine into a global ranking.

**Critical quality warning:** OpenAlex's `article` label and primary journal field do not reliably distinguish an original law-review article from a book review, student note, comment, foreword or other item. Some records appear to conflate a book with a review and attach the book's citation graph or authors to the journal entry. For example, *The Death and Life of Great American Cities* appears under the Yale source with both review and book author names, and *A Monetary History of the United States* appears under Chicago similarly. These are flagged conceptually as unverified leads; the reported citation counts cannot safely be attributed to the law-review items. This is a provider-level limitation across the entire set, not a few cleanly isolated rows. Check the actual law-review table of contents, authored item, full publication citation and source/DOI relationship, then confirm title, authors, item type and count against a legal-specific source before making a shortlist or computing any distribution.

[Primary legal ranking sources and separate legal forms](ranking-sources.md) identifies the 2012 Shapiro–Pearse article and the current HeinOnline ranking, explains the important difference between HeinOnline and OpenAlex counts, and lists courts, briefs and OLC materials. No data from HeinOnline have been assigned to the OpenAlex records. Neither bibliographic count nor article popularity establishes writing quality or present legal validity. No legal advice is intended.

## HeinOnline's separate top-100 snapshot and screening

`heinonline-top100.json` and `.csv` record the 100 rows of [HeinOnline's public rendered ranking](https://access.heinonline.com/HOL/scholarly-impact-ranking/comprehensive-articles) observed 16 November 2026. Each row retains its own rank, journal citation and date as printed, author as printed, 0–5-year article citations, 5+-year article citations, cumulative citations and past-12-month accesses. This is a **separate HeinOnline cohort**, not a crosswalk or replacement count for any OpenAlex entry. Dates are preserved as printed, including volume-year spans. Rank 38 has no author printed in the captured table. All citation columns passed the check `0–5 + 5+ = cumulative` on the captured rows.

The local HTTP fetch returned 403; the public web viewer rendered 307 lines including 100 rows, which were transformed into structured metadata on that date. Thus the files are a snapshot of the rendered table; raw HTML was not locally acquired and there is no local HTML hash or executable direct-fetch reproduction. Ranking and access figures will drift; inspect the provider page to refresh. `heinonline-candidates.csv` holds 97 rows after three obvious or possible out-of-scope/genre conflicts are held: two *International Organization* entries and a probable book review. These holds are narrow and do not confirm the remaining article types; forewords, comments and other forms remain and need source-level review.

The 390 OpenAlex discovery records are preserved, with `known_or_suspected_conflict` and `hold_reason` added for 44 known book-title or probable book/review conflations. `library-candidates.csv` excludes these 44, leaving 346 **unscreened** leads; this is a partial hold list, not a claim that the remaining records are correctly classified or that OpenAlex's counts belong to the journal items. Verify the original item and attribution before requesting or analyzing it.
