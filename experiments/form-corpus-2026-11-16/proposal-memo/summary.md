Institutional example selection is a signal of educational relevance, not a citation ranking or proof of universal quality.

The six decision memos are individual recommendation-bearing memoranda from the State Department's *Foreign Relations of the United States* (FRUS), selected by its historical editors. `candidates.json` records each actual decision task, discovery query and relevance, and `metrics.json` records titles, dates, and source hashes. Their printed documentary titles identify authors and recipients. These are all historical U.S. executive-branch foreign-policy decisions, an intentionally narrow and convenience-selected collection. Historical significance and editorial inclusion are selection signals, not verified citation counts or a claim that these are the most-read examples. Source URLs, source titles, and dates are recorded per document; date of retrieval is 2026-11-16. Treat U.S. Government source material as subject to the source's own rights and attribution policy; rights in any third-party or archival materials have not been independently established. ## Measurement and limits

`python3 experiments/form-corpus-2026-11-16/proposal-memo/measure.py` downloads or reuses the six cached official HTML files, checks HTTP status and document structure, calculates SHA-256 over retrieved HTML, and emits `metrics.json`. A memo unit is the published transcription of the primary memorandum in its FRUS document, not every referenced attachment: missing/unprinted supporting tabs and editorial footnotes are excluded. These are complete **published primary-text units**, not claims that all original attachments or redacted passages are available. One must review the source notes and compare scans before treating any item as the complete original package.

The script separates paragraphs, dash-led/list items, explicit approve/disapprove choice lines, and recognized section headings; it excludes the title, date/routing opener, subject, page markers and editorial footnotes. It reports main-body words including recognized list and choice text, prose-only sentence and paragraph counts, and average word-character length. It retains inline references where they are part of the text but removes editorial footnote markers. It does not infer authorial citations, examples, section function, figures, quality, success, or reproducible section proportions. Approximate sentence boundaries and HTML-derived paragraphs can be wrong: abbreviations, numbered text, embedded lists, signatures, and section labels presented as styled paragraphs can be misclassified; some choice text embedded in a longer paragraph remains prose. Heading counts are specifically recognized markup, not a full functional-outline count. These are provisional machine measurements, not a manually audited transcription.

The six measured body lengths are 378–2,074 words with a median of 1,289.5 words; these figures describe this small selected sample and are not writing limits or targets. The collection is too small, historically clustered, and unranked to establish general conventions. NSF-specific requirements and the institutional policy-memo guidance in the project's authority crosswalk describe scoped practices, not empirical general-purpose length limits.

## Next steps

Inspect PDF text and boundaries with an available trusted PDF extractor, respecting each sample's permissions, then manually confirm proposal narratives and distinguish narrative from cover, budget, references, and annotations. Expand discovery using bounded, documented funder and institutional collections and record the full denominator and screening decisions. For decision memos, sample multiple periods and recipient/decision settings; individually inspect whether the request, options and recommendation are present, whether the published unit is complete, and whether source or editorial annotations are included. Review parser boundaries on representative files before interpreting section or prose distributions. Confirm individual notability with traceable signals rather than equating FRUS inclusion with citation rank.
