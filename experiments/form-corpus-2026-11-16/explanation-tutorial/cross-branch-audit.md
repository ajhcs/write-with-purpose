# Bounded independent cross-branch audit

16 November 2026. Read-only examination of the other branches' saved records, scripts, reports, and a few source caches; no network requests or full reruns. This is a spot audit, not an endorsement or an estimate of collection-wide error. A source-typed or mechanically accepted candidate is not a verified member of its proposed form.

## Material findings

1. **Empirical main prose can include administrative sections.** In `empirical-methods/manifest.csv`, PMC1904193 has 2,696 `main_words`, including 10 words under “Competing interests,” 96 under “Authors' contributions,” and 10 under “Pre-publication history.” The cached XML has these as body sections and `collect.py` traverses every top-level body section. The cached abstract does identify a randomized trial, so this example supports its empirical classification but not a claim that all counted words are substantive main text. The first and third sampled empirical records did not show those three administrative labels in their recorded section details; that is not evidence that they have no other extraction problems.

2. **Narrative-review body words can include administrative sections.** In `narrative-scoping/narrative-metrics.json`, PMC10954893 has 10,856 body words. Its XML body sections include Acknowledgements (12), Author contributions (37), Funding (20), Data availability (19), Declarations (35), Footnotes (17), and Associated Data (19): 159 words counted in the output. The References section contributes zero because the parser removes `ref-list` elements. This is a source-layout issue, not proof of a systematic rate across the sample. The metadata `year: 2024` is the Europe PMC publication-year field; cached JATS gives a 28 July 2023 publication date. Preserve the distinction rather than presenting 2024 as verified first-publication date.

3. **Some measured-source digests are not in durable records.** `empirical-methods/collect.py` calculates `xml_sha256`, `xml_retrieved_utc`, and `citation_snapshot_utc`, but the current `manifest.csv` header omits them. Three sampled XML files exist in the local cache, but a manifest consumer cannot compare a record's source bytes to its original recorded digest. The sampled narrative metrics and candidate record likewise have no XML hash although cached XML exists. A cache can support further audit, but existence is not proof of original byte identity or immutability. Critical-theory records hash extracted paragraph text, not raw XML, and say the source text was not retained. Procedure-reference records hash HTML responses, while the collector states it does not save page text. The latter two cannot be independently read back from retained raw source in this sample without reacquisition.

4. **Procedure/reference individual `accepted` fields are not final inclusion decisions.** `procedure-records.jsonl` sets `accepted: true` on “CSS layout cookbook,” and `reference-records.jsonl` does so on “ARIA states and properties (attributes).” Both are explicitly excluded by the later title-based reject sets in `summarize.py`; they are not in the reported selected totals. Filtering the JSONL by `accepted` alone therefore does not reproduce the summary. The branch report correctly describes a separate title screen; consumers need that screen or an explicit per-record final inclusion flag. A title-based screen still cannot confirm page purpose.

5. **Source labels are known to misclassify a sampled article.** The first `essays/measurements.jsonl` candidate, PMC3935836, has a Personal Narrative publication type but its cached JATS title and body identify a systematic review and meta-analysis with Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion sections. It is marked `not_manually_screened`; do not interpret its measurement as an accepted reflective essay. The critical-theory collection independently records PMC3664238 as a `book-review`-labeled candidate despite its penetrance-research title and absent reviewed-product metadata; it is appropriately not counted as a source-label-confirmed work review. These examples show why the provisional filters matter.

6. **Record order is not citation rank.** The critical-theory discovery requests citation sorting, but `collect.py` writes records in `as_completed` order: its first three recorded citation counts are 299, 39, and 63. The essays records are likewise completion-ordered. Use recorded source rank where provided, citation provider/snapshot and scoped query; do not treat JSONL row order as citation rank or call any set globally most cited. The public Paul Graham archive records archive order explicitly, which is not readership order.

## Spot checks and scope

- Procedure/reference: read the first three mechanically accepted titles in each output and the title rejection code; no original HTML cache is retained by this collector, so no source-content or navigation audit was possible without a network reacquisition.
- Essays: read PMC3935836 cached XML title and section headings, and the cached HTML of “How to Lose Time and Money” (author/date and opening content are consistent with its record). These examples do not validate other entries or parser completeness.
- Critical-theory: inspected the first candidate and sampled a measured book-review and a publisher-category conceptual record; no source XML was retained in that branch. Discovery order and source-hash scope were inspected in code.
- Empirical/methods: checked the first three empirical XML caches and recorded section details; all three cached XML roots are `research-article`. Only the second was examined for abstract and administrative-section content.
- Narrative/scoping: checked the first narrative candidate's cached XML, publication dates, section labels, and recorded measures. No scoping-specific unit was examined.

No correction was applied to another branch. These findings are extraction and provenance qualifications, not writing targets or estimates of form-wide conventions.
