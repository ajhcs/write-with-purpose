# Library retrieval queue: citation-ranked candidates

**Scope:** this queue comes from biomedical-oriented Europe PMC searches. It is a medical/health discovery baseline, not a balanced cross-domain library request. The author's requested [business, engineering, law and personal-writing expansion](../domain-expansion/README.md) should be consulted alongside it. Similar form names across disciplines do not make the sources interchangeable.

The discovery register contains 400 unique DOI records selected from Europe PMC metadata on 16 November 2026: 200 first published through 2014 (P1) and 200 from 2015–2019 (P2), initially 25 per form cue per period. **26 known or possible type conflicts are held, leaving 374 provisional retrieval candidates: 186 P1 and 188 P2.** None is a confirmed form exemplar or a measured document.

`library-first.csv` contains the 275 unheld candidates Europe PMC marks `inPMC=N`: 147 P1 and 128 P2. Both held records and those marked `inPMC=Y` remain in the full discovery register with their status visible. **All 400 have `isOpenAccess=N` in Europe PMC metadata.** Neither flag proves paywall status, absence of an accessible version, lack of a full-text archive elsewhere, or library entitlement. Provider links can even label an outside location free; those labels are retained as unverified metadata. No article full texts were requested, inspected, or measured here. Records are not represented as unavailable through an archive merely because they are in the library-first subset.

- `queue.csv` / `queue.json`: full discovery metadata, exact query, rank, citation counts with timestamp, provider source type, access flags, DOI, classification basis, and screening status. Each held record has `known_type_conflict=true` and a specific `hold_reason`; the field also covers precautionary title-cue holds that are not confirmed conflicts; `eligible-candidates.csv` contains the 374 unheld records. The WHOQOL and Scandinavian Stroke Study Group protocol titles have no author string in the returned metadata; consult the publisher before using their attribution.
- `shortlist.md`: the first ten unheld candidates per form and period, linked by DOI, with provider-specific counts, first-publication date and indexed publication year. The date used to allocate a period is the first-publication date, which may differ from the indexed publication year. It includes both PMC flags; use `library-first.csv` to filter for the defined library-first subset.
- `receipt.json`: reproducible request URLs, query hit counts, page counts, filter reasons, response hashes, and list of earlier local metadata files consulted. Search responses are cached outside this repository in `/workspace/repos/builderbob/.writing-corpus-cache/library-queue/`.
- `sources/library-ranking-resources.md`: official provider documentation, usage-versus-citation distinctions, and legitimate library retrieval workflow.

Reproduce with `python3 experiments/form-corpus-2026-11-16/library-queue/build.py`. It uses cached provider responses when present; deleting or updating the cache changes the snapshot and is not required for reproduction. The logged per-query timestamp derives from the cached response file's modification time, and the receipt records a separate rebuild time. The first 100 hits per query were requested, and the first 25 surviving each query's local screen were selected. Categories are processed in the script's order, so duplicates are assigned to the first eligible category. Across the 400 discovery DOI records no duplicate DOI occurs. The type-conflict screen is applied after discovery selection and held records are not replaced in the 400-record register; where possible the shortlist takes the next unheld record from that existing selection.

The Europe PMC query excludes `Retracted Publication` and titles containing “retracted”; the returned publication types and titles are also screened for retraction terms. That is a metadata-only screen, **not** confirmation that none has subsequently been retracted, corrected or otherwise challenged. Check the current publisher and retraction record before use.

Prior corpus files present at build time were conservatively scanned for DOI, PMCID and PMID; identifiers appearing in those files were excluded whether they were merely discovered or had been measured. `receipt.json` lists the files actually scanned. This reduces overlap with the earlier XML work but cannot prove that there is no overlap with unlisted caches, records added later, or identifier variants.

## Meaning and known classification risks

A local title screen holds identified PRISMA reporting statements, explanations, checklists and protocol/reporting guidance from systematic-synthesis retrieval. A documented DOI-specific screen also holds clear non-case documents, methods or definitions indexed as trial reports, and results or author-group matches indexed under study-protocol cues. The 26 holds are intentionally transparent in the full register and are excluded from both retrieval CSVs and the shortlist. This is an incomplete screen of obvious conflicts, not confirmation of the remaining 374 classifications.

All form labels are provisional. Exact title phrases were verified literally after provider search for six title-cued groups; the provider returned seven pre-2015 and one later study-protocol matches without the literal phrase, which the local screen excluded. “Theoretical framework” can appear in a review or application paper, and “new method” can be a tool-development, applied, or clinical methods paper. `Case Reports` and `Randomized Controlled Trial` are index types, not guarantees of primary document form. For example, the high-ranked epilepsy definition carries the Case Reports type but is an official report; the high-ranked Bland–Altman methods paper carries the Randomized Controlled Trial type but its title describes a methods contribution. Both are held as known or possible type conflicts. Other remaining candidates need full-text screening before any inclusion in case or trial distributions.

Citation counts are Europe PMC indexed citation snapshots, sorted within the precise query and period; they are neither field-normalized nor globally most-cited. Restricting the search to English, biomedical-oriented indexing, specific title/type cues, and provider access flags affects coverage. A high count is a discovery signal, not evidence of readable prose, causal influence, or exemplar quality. Observed counts cannot be converted into writing targets or editorial limits.
