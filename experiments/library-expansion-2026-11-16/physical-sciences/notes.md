# Physical sciences and mathematics retrieval targets

Search snapshot: 2026-11-16. Targeted searches used Crossref exact DOI records, OpenAlex DOI records, APS and AIP publisher pages, CERN, Caltech, Stanford, Nobel Prize and NIST records. This is a targeted selection, not an exhaustive systematic search. OpenAlex cited-by counts are provider-specific snapshots, not readership or global rank. Archive, institutional publication and curated publisher collections are distinct selection signals; they do not imply high citation counts. No full-text PDFs were downloaded.

The three selected autobiographical pieces are first-person accounts: Feynman's reminiscence explicitly recounts his Los Alamos experience, while the Nobel biographies recount Cornell's and Ginzburg's lives and scientific development. These replace the earlier technical Nobel lectures. Their Nobel autobiographies share the title “Biographical”; author and institutional URL distinguish them. The three Nature items are reviews of books, selected as individual review articles, not as requests to retrieve the books themselves. The complete FASER technical proposal is a 2018 supplement, as are the 2015 Barz tutorial and 2016 gravitational-wave paper. Original CERN proposal records may present browser restrictions; the FASER preprint and ATLAS official document listing provide additional locators. The NIST items are individual named SOPs embedded within longer compilations; retrieve the named SOP only. The reference examples are individually authored Stanford encyclopedia entries on philosophical foundations of physics, with named historical editions and no asserted DOI.

The 1945 Franck memorandum is a scientist-authored policy recommendation, with no DOI asserted. The June 11 report and June 12 covering memorandum must not be conflated. Conditional identification and archival title variation are retained in the candidate's limitations. Two other decision memos remain unfilled: targeted searches of the National Security Archive and University of Chicago sources surfaced official or military cover notes and summaries, which do not independently satisfy the scientist-authored decision memo requirement.

The case-report, study/review-protocol and scoping-review cells remain empty after targeted search. A particle-physics experiment report is not a case report; a proposed experiment or detector is not a standalone study/review protocol. Searches for "physics education scoping review 2019" and "physics education protocol systematic review 2019" did not produce a defensible pre-2020 candidate with the explicit form and selection signal. For systematic review, the APS 2015 meta-analysis https://journals.aps.org/prper/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevSTPER.11.010115 reports synthesis of 24 physics-education studies and defines its instruments; its abstract does not establish a sufficiently explicit systematic search and screening method, so it remains a lead rather than being assigned to the cell. A later confirmed systematic physics education review (2023) is outside the requested period. These are search gaps, not assertions that such examples do not exist.

Three topical reviews are narrative syntheses; no claim is made that they use systematic or scoping methods. Tutorials psm_10 and psm_11 have relatively modest OpenAlex citation counts. The reference entries, procedures, and technical proposals are selected as official institutional examples rather than asserted citation leaders; especially for the procedure cell, review them against the user's desired prominence threshold.
