The catalogue permits overlapping functions; the readings below are analytical choices, not mutually exclusive labels. The quoted anchors identify evidence for a reading. Realization spans give the complete text used for that reading.

- **report** — Anchor: “The proposed rule says”. Realization: “The proposed rule says that every delayed task is low priority.” The sentence reports a proposed universal rule; reporting it does not endorse it. It also **orients** the reader to the proposition under challenge.
- **illustrate** — Anchor: “an urgent approval”. Realization: “Consider an urgent approval that arrives on Friday when no authorized person is available until Monday.” This is a hypothetical case, not an observed event or evidence of a population rate.
- **interpret** and **assert** — Anchor: “would not establish low priority”. Realization: “That possible delay would not establish low priority.” The sentence draws and asserts a limited inference about the imagined delay.
- **support** — Anchor: “every delayed task” and “That possible delay would not establish low priority.” Realization: the entire paragraph. The imagined urgent approval supplies a possible counterexample to the universal rule. This is reasoning from a hypothetical, not empirical case evidence. The scenario and the conclusion may also be read together as **explain**, because they make the challenge to the rule intelligible.

Relations: The hypothetical scenario **illustrates** a possible delayed urgent task. The final inference **follows** from the scenario and **supports** the challenge to the reported rule; the hypothetical's urgency **contrasts** with the rule's assignment of low priority. These are local analytical readings, not a claim of a complete formal discourse parse.
