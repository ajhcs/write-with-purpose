## Decisive corrections and unresolved reporting

**P03: distinguish evaluation measures before alleging an inconsistency.** The funding review contrasts Table 2's 2.4% with Figure 4's 2.24%. Physical PDF p. 5, Figure 3 distinguishes cross-validation error from 80/20 train/test prediction error and reports approximately 2.41% and 2.24%, respectively, for α = 1. Table 2 can round the former to 2.4%, while Figure 4 shows the latter value. The p. 6 surrounding prose is not completely explicit about the basis of every figure; therefore this is an **ambiguous metric basis**, not an established contradiction in the same estimate. Both results are retrospective, potentially affected by post-outcome language and controls; neither supports prelaunch causal claims or adopting winning phrases.

**P09: table and prose differ but their cause is unresolved.** Direct visual readback of physical p. 4 confirms Table 2's confidence entries 62.36% easiest and 48.78% hardest, and understanding entries 57.87%, 39.47%, 40.79%, 47.95%; Figure 1 identifies 170 participants for confidence and 143 with sufficient understanding data, yielding 628 item responses. Physical p. 5 reports confidence 58.3% and 44.1% for accessible and traditional styles. The paper does not clearly reconcile these values in the passages inspected; do not silently choose one, call a cause established, or mistake the table layout for the error. Its modeled comprehension effect comes from bundled writing changes, not isolated ingredients.

**P04: an actual definition reversal appears in the text.** Visual inspection of physical p. 5 confirms the text says impressions divided by clicks while defining its binomial dependent variable. The paper's p. 3 defines click-through as clicks divided by impressions, so the two statements conflict. Treat p. 5 as a reporting issue; it does not alone prove which calculation generated the model. The interaction on p. 6 is −0.058, and its 35,910 non-unique headline observations are not 35,910 independent randomized experiments.

**P11: table-vs-conclusion scope matters.** Visual inspection of physical p. 10, Table 10 confirms first-stage abstract tentative-word coefficient 0.024, p = .025, N = 2,493, while the adjacent discussion broadly says linguistic features of abstracts did not affect scores and decisions. This is a real tension if read as a blanket null statement; it does not establish that tentative wording caused improved scoring, and should not be treated as an externally replicated finding. Its separate stage-two project-description result uses N = 677, not the abstract Table 10's stage-two N = 682.

**Other reported inconsistencies remain bounded.** P01 p. 7 reports 109 users out of 330 offered definitions and labels this 31% (arithmetic approximately 33%); that discrepancy does not alter the randomized wording assignment. P10 p. 1 states OR 1.47 while p. 6 figure displays 1.46 with the same displayed interval; preserve the difference without asserting a cause. P13 p. 3 text says 53,699 headlines and its figure caption says 53,669; the signed complexity coefficient in prose differs from its negative z and confidence interval on p. 4. These do not authorize repairing a model or choosing a hidden number. P18 p. 12 gives 236 + 671 = 907 while saying 908 units; the remaining unit's status is unspecified. Its Figure 3 says 27 studies reported an agreement type, while p. 11 says 28 clearly reported results: different wording could imply different coding, so retain as unresolved, not established error.

## Independent checks across themes

- **Audience:** P01 pp. 6–7 and 10 support N = 650 randomized participants and perceived fluency 4.57 with jargon versus 5.27 without. Optional mouseovers and self-report do not test all possible definitions or objective comprehension. P09's actual table and participant flow were visually checked as above.
- **Attention:** P04 pp. 5–6 reports 8,977 same-image bundles and 35,910 headline observations; its paired tests randomize entire packages, while concreteness is a measured feature. P07 pp. 9–10 confirms 5,048 exploratory and 24,333 held-out selected pairs and 54.42% winner-prediction accuracy; this is conditional on the paper's selection. P13 p. 3 describes 12,448 experimental tests and p. 3–4 reports the adjusted 2.3% relative click-through association for one added negative word. P04, P07 and P13 all use the historical Upworthy archive; overlapping populations cannot be treated as independent replications. Individual feature coefficients do not isolate causal word substitutions, even where packages were randomized.
- **Narrative and style:** P06 p. 3 states 6,956 homepage articles and a 2,566-item coded subsample; p. 10 reports self-reported sharing intentions 3.97 versus 2.92 for the different high/low-amusement advertisement versions. That is not observed sharing and cannot isolate an emotion from every changed story detail. P08 pp. 1–2 gives 732 screened climate abstracts, with narrative/citation association confounded with journal identity; citations are not demonstrated comprehension. P12 p. 10 reports Study 4 F(1,232) and F(1,200) for two outcomes and calls its tense manipulation personal voice in the mediation paragraph; no missing-data explanation was established here.
- **Funding:** P03 p. 3 confirms 45,810 ended Kickstarter campaigns; its retrospective models include potentially post-outcome material. P10 pp. 1 and 6 and P11 p. 10 were checked as above. Statistical association, propensity score or cross-validation does not turn an edited phrase into a demonstrated cause of funding.
- **Genre and moves:** P16 p. 5 confirms 22 authentic reports, 12 external and 10 internal, with source-specific 409–28,593-word range and median 4,614. P17 pp. 3 and 10 confirms 900 Methods sections across 30 disciplines and 94,925 functional annotation units. Those units, documents and disciplines are distinct denominators. Descriptive genre conventions supply comparisons, not required steps or tested quality gains.
- **Interactive persuasion:** The P02 recovered author-copy pp. 2 and 5 confirms 3,456 training and 807 held-out matched pairs and 65.1% versus 59.6% pairwise prediction; authors explicitly disavow causal explanation. Pairs share discussion context and are not independently randomized rewrites. A delta measures an original poster's reported view change, not factual accuracy or enduring persuasion.
- **Agreement:** P18 p. 7 confirms nine training introductions (258 sentence units), then 26 introductions (908 sentence units), out of the 35 recoded introductions, and p. 1 identifies the 600-introduction COSSRAI parent corpus. Sentences are the fixed scoring units even when conceptual rhetorical chunks cross them. Its α and κ compare trained human sentence labels; our 21/36 vs 18/36 are exact sets on selected paragraphs from different model pairs. Neither comparison determines semantic correctness, and shared empty sets can inflate layer agreement. No 750-unit universal threshold follows.

## Rule and reuse boundaries

The current writing skills already protect reader purpose, accurate referents, technical vocabulary, supported qualifications and author voice; the supplied studies mostly reaffirm or suggest testing those commitments. Search-retrievability, click-through, reader confidence, correct answers, funding and citation counts are distinct outcomes. No reviewed result establishes a universal readability grade, required headline sentiment, fixed move sequence, maximum length, mandatory single paragraph function or winning phrase. Word-level flags, offsets, source IDs and explicit structural fields can be checked mechanically; whether a passage or example satisfies a reader's purpose, supports a claim, preserves nuance or sounds like the author needs contextual review and author judgment.

The existing 159 crosswalk entries and downloaded datasets can supply original source units, labels, examples and plausible coding challenges; they are not established paragraph-function labels. GUM relation direction and secondary edges, legal actor attribution, README section roles, scientific sentence roles and Webis argumentative units remain distinct layers. Astra's proposed durable idea provenance, reversible choices and reader-focused assistance are plausible differentiators to test against the same base model and existing skills, not demonstrated superiority. No canonical rule, installed skill or original source was changed in this audit.
