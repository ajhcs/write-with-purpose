# Interactive persuasion: full-paper review

Chenhao Tan, Vlad Niculae, Cristian Danescu-Niculescu-Mizil and Lillian Lee, “[Winning Arguments: Interaction Dynamics and Persuasion Strategies in Good-faith Online Discussions](https://doi.org/10.1145/2872427.2883081)” (WWW, 2016), is an observational corpus and predictive study, not an experiment or evidence that a writing edit causes persuasion. I read all twelve pages, including the appendix and references, of the recovered [Cornell author copy](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~cristian/pdfs/winning_arguments.pdf); PDF pages below refer to that copy. The supplied attachment has corrupt streams and blank pages 1 and 4 under two extractors. [Recovery receipt](../P02-recovery.json) retains source and SHA-256; the incomplete original extraction was not treated as complete.

The outcome is an original poster's recorded delta acknowledging some view change in a moderated, self-selected Reddit community whose posters invite challenge (pp. 2–3). That is a useful participant-reported immediate proxy; it does not measure truth, comprehension, durable belief change or behavior. Delta allocation, attention, reply visibility and dialogue are not controlled by random assignment. The authors explicitly disclaim causal explanations (p. 2), and discuss limits of generalizing to other environments and behavior (p. 10).

## Design and results

The 2013–2015 archive contains 18,363 training discussion trees and 2,263 later heldout trees (1,114,533 and 145,733 nodes respectively; Table 1, p. 3). These are not all independent writing samples for every task. For the argument comparison, each delta-winning rooted path is paired to the unsuccessful path in the same tree with highest stopword-removed root-reply Jaccard similarity. Selection requires root replies of at least 50 words, at least ten challengers and three unsuccessful paths before the OP's last reply. The data comprise 3,456 training and 807 later heldout pairs (p. 5). Pairs approximate lexical/topic similarity but do not equate reasoning, evidence, factuality, author, exposure or timing. Original post matching narrows some confounds; it does not isolate wording.

In that selected, balanced task, interplay features attain 65.1% pair accuracy versus 59.6% bag-of-words on the 807 heldout pairs (pp. 2, 5, 8); the paper reports approximately 60% for the root-reply word-count baseline (p. 8). These are pair-choice results, not 65.1% of all arguments persuading anyone. Full-path features include later exchanges and therefore cannot be treated as an opening-only prediction. The authors controlled length *after* matching in a separate root-truncated analysis by cutting the longer reply; this can delete important text, alter distributions and reverse signs, and absence of significance there is not proof a feature is false (pp. 5–6).

Content-word overlap normalized by reply length was lower in delta-winning replies relative to the original post within these similar pairs; untruncated stopword similarity sometimes went the other way, and several signs reversed after truncation (Table 2, p. 6). This does not justify deliberately varying words, mimicking a reader's stopwords, or attributing a psychological state to them. In the argument-only analysis, hedge cues and first- and second-person pronouns had higher **raw counts** in successful root replies and full paths; Table 3 does not mark those differences as surviving truncation (pp. 6, 10). Definite and indefinite article counts both track length; only definite-article fraction is marked significant after truncation (pp. 6, 10). Links were associated in both count and normalized form and retained significance after truncation, but source credibility, relevance and causation were not established. An example-marker count is not an annotation of evidence quality. Arousal was lower, while concreteness and dominance showed no significant difference in the argument comparison; Flesch–Kincaid did not differ significantly in root replies, but did in full paths (p. 7). These results are feature associations, not instructions to add hedges, articles, pronouns, links, or length.

Interaction strongly conditions the outcome: earlier challengers were more likely to get a delta; among paths with six or more challenger replies, 0 of 129 won one, and with five replies the paper reports 1 in approximately 3,000 (Fig. 4, p. 4). The relation was nonmonotonic, not evidence that engagement or stopping at a fixed turn causes success. The proportion of posts with a delta rose sublinearly with unique challenger count; among subtrees of two to four replies, single-challenger subtrees did better than multiple-challenger ones (Fig. 5, pp. 4–5). Selection and reverse causality remain plausible: weak arguments may attract additional challengers, long debates may reflect persistent disagreement, and exposure changes with entry.

A different task predicts whether a post receives any delta: 10,743 selected training and 1,529 heldout OPs, each with at least ten challengers and one OP reply (pp. 8–9). AUC was about 0.54 in the heldout setting (p. 2); the task does not diagnose an individual's openness. In these posts, first-person pronouns associated with a delta and first-person plural with no delta; the authors cite contrary prior work and offer self-affirmation as an interpretation, not an experimentally demonstrated mechanism (p. 9). Topic-model features did not add predictive power in their tested tasks, and sequential discourse connectors performed poorly (pp. 9–10). Do not infer attitude or receptivity from pronouns.

## Implications for our rules and experiments

- **IP-01 — reaffirm and refine context:** `skills/write-with-purpose/SKILL.md` already organizes around the reader's question and retains precise terms and reason links. When an author explicitly seeks to respond to a known position, record the intended audience, their stated claim, author purpose and relevant source/context separately; use these to ask whether the response addresses the claim. Mechanical fields can preserve supplied actors, source identifiers and target spans; whether a response fairly engages a position is semantic review. Counterexample: an exploratory or personal piece may have no known opposing claim or persuasion goal. This is a candidate workflow hypothesis, not demonstrated advantage.
- **IP-02 — reaffirm scoped qualification:** `skills/write-with-purpose/SKILL.md` and `skills/edit-with-purpose/references/plain-language-edit.md` preserve meaningful qualifications. The raw hedge-count finding does not justify deleting or adding hedges globally. Check the actual uncertainty and claim scope, preserving the author's intended assertion. Counterexample: a redundant hedge can obscure a well-established fact; a necessary “may” can prevent a misleading certainty.
- **IP-03 — refine actor/target annotation without collapsing layers:** `docs/writing-harness/research/astra-rule-revision-2026-11-16.md` and `experiments/dataset-adoption-2026-11-16/plugin-reuse-notes.md` already distinguish speaker, addressee and relation targets. A reply's author, original poster, and original post are distinct actor, audience and target records; the reply edge is not automatically a support/attack function label. Store supplied source links and unresolved targets mechanically; identify actual argumentative function and scope through review. Counterexample: quoted “you” may address a character, not the reader. The corpus supplies interaction metadata, not paragraph-function gold.
- **IP-04 — do not adopt a persuasion optimizer:** Neither this paper nor current foundations justify prescribing pronouns, link density, word count or lexical dissimilarity to maximize delta rates. Preserve author agency, qualifications and purpose. A counterexample is a technical explanation whose essential terminology must remain consistent, or a safety warning whose urgency is warranted.

The local 36-paragraph calibration compared original exact-set agreement **21/36** and candidate revision **18/36**, with no human gold or validated function-length spans. This paper did not test our labels, tools or readers and cannot establish improvement, semantic accuracy or a new numeric target. A real differentiator remains a testable hypothesis: preserve author-supplied audience, actor and claim-target provenance while helping the author inspect alternatives. Common conversation metadata and analysis tools already exist; no comparative advantage has been measured.

## Data and code reuse

The [original January 2016 README](https://chenhaot.com/data/cmv/README.txt) documents a 321 MB archive with separate train/heldout `all/`, `pair_task/` and `op_task/` JSONL-bzip2 files. Pair records contain `op_author`, `op_text`, `op_name`, `op_title`, `positive`, and `negative`. The [author's code walkthrough](https://vene.ro/blog/winning-arguments-attitude-change-reddit-cmv.html) shows loading and a simplified cleanup/classifier; it explicitly says its cleanup differs from the original research preprocessing. Do not claim that example replicates all paper results. The author's [project page](https://chenhaot.com/papers/changemyview/) also links a later November 2016 update; retain release identity.

[ConvoKit's converted subset](https://convokit.cornell.edu/documentation/winning.html) documents 3,051 conversations, 293,297 utterances and 34,911 speakers for its stated training-period subset; `speaker`, `reply_to`, `conversation_id`, timestamps, `success` and `pair_ids` support an adapter. It notes 530 comments missing text and author, reused arguments in multiple pairs, and `success = null` for other comments. Preserve those missing values and group by conversation and author when evaluating; do not count related pairs as independent. The [GitHub notebook](https://github.com/CornellNLP/ConvoKit/blob/master/datasets/winning-args-corpus/stats.ipynb) is a selection/example lead, not a verified full paper replication. Dataset, Reddit text, annotations, code and dependency licenses/permissions require separate review. No data or package was downloaded or installed.
