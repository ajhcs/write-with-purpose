# Agreement and empirical writing rules: full-text review

Both papers argue against treating a simple surface measure as an answer to a harder writing question. Kim, Qiu and Wang examine agreement on rhetorical labels; Weinberger, Evans and Allesina examine citation associations with abstract features. Neither tests whether our twelve functions are semantically correct, whether readers understand a revision, or whether a writing change causes more citations. The candidate implications below leave accepted rules and prior results unchanged. The corresponding [structured record](agreement-writing-rules.json) includes all ten writing-rule proxies and source locators.

## P18: Interrater agreement in genre analysis

Kim, Qiu and Wang (2024), [DOI 10.1016/j.rmal.2024.100097](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rmal.2024.100097), is a systematic methodological review plus a separate empirical comparison of agreement measures, not a meta-analysis. PDF pages below correspond to printed pages. The supplied full text, including methods, results, conclusion and appendices, was read.

The search returned 1,430 Google Scholar and 341 LLBA records; the authors selected 65 manually annotated research-article genre studies published from 2003 through January 2023. Three researchers independently coded the 65 papers using a 16-item review instrument, followed by reconciliation (p. 6 and Appendix C, p. 16). Figure 3 reports 38/65 did not report an agreement type (p. 8). Nine of 65 discussed multifunctional units, six of those nine describing treatment (p. 10). Non-reporting is not proof the authors did not conduct a procedure. The authors separately report 28 studies clearly reporting agreement results (p. 11), whereas Figure 3 identifies 27 reporting a type; the difference in definitions or accounting is unresolved.

For their metric comparison, the three experienced doctoral researchers used nine introductions comprising 258 sentence units for training and calibration, then independently coded 26 introductions comprising 908 sentence units (p. 7). The rhetorical chunk is the conceptual unit; each sentence is the fixed annotation unit. A function extending across sentences receives a code on each sentence, while different functions within one sentence can receive multiple codes. This design does **not** independently test exact sub-sentence boundaries or establish the full realization span for each function. It is a human-coded, social-science introduction sample; the wider corpus was supplied by a researcher, and access terms for the underlying texts and labels are unspecified (pp. 1, 7, 14).

Percent agreement counts exact unit agreement and does not correct for chance; Cohen and Fleiss kappa use chance-adjusted single-label agreement and are sensitive to the coding margins (pp. 3–4). For nominal sets, the paper defines disagreement using Dice distance, `1 − 2|C ∩ K|/(|C| + |K|)`; Table 1 assigns two empty sets zero disagreement. Multi-valued alpha adjusts observed disagreement for expected disagreement and ignores label order (pp. 4–5). It gives partial credit for overlapping sets, unlike exact-set agreement. It requires an explicitly chosen unit and a defensible treatment of missing or unresolved annotations: an observed empty set is not the same as an unassessed unit. The paper does not establish the missing-item treatment actually used in its sample.

The results demonstrate why the measure and label granularity matter. Among the 908 evaluated sentence units, at least one rater assigned multiple labels to 236 (25.99%); the authors also report 671 as monofunctional throughout (p. 12, Fig. 8). Those reported counts sum to 907, leaving one unit unaccounted for in the prose; its status is not explained. Agreement fell with a change from three move categories to 14 broad steps and then 22 fine steps. One coder pair had higher multi-valued alpha than single-label kappa because they shared functions but differed in their primary/secondary ordering; other pairs behaved differently (p. 11, Fig. 6). The authors report that the difference between AC1 and kappa was about 2% once curves leveled at 750 units in this particular dataset (p. 12, Fig. 7). They recommend larger samples and more than two raters (p. 13), but 750 is not a general minimum, proof of stability in document-clustered data, or an acceptance threshold for our work. Their claim that multi-valued alpha is most accurate is stronger than what their comparison alone establishes: they also acknowledge no consistent practical superiority over other measures (p. 13), and no independent semantic gold standard is measured.

The study reports its review-extraction coding separately: average 95.38% agreement, Fleiss kappa 0.94 and alpha 0.94 across review fields, with three researchers coding 65 articles (p. 6). Those are not scores for the rhetorical labels. Appendix B lists the reviewed studies and Appendix C gives the review's 16-item coding guide (pp. 15–16). R and separate Java software were used for the metrics (p. 7); project-specific scripts, the three-rater matrix and source-text rights are not supplied in this text.

### Local comparison and candidates

The [fresh calibration report](../../rule-calibration-2026-11-16/report.md) reports original clear exact-set agreement **21/36** and revised **18/36**, with different independent model pairs per guidance arm. P18 evaluates trained human raters on sentence-anchored research introductions. These measures, sampling units and populations differ; no kappa or alpha can be reconstructed from the headline totals. Neither result establishes accuracy. Our relation-layer **29/36** exact figure includes **21 shared empty sets**; temporal-layer **35/36** includes **27 shared empty sets**. Those denominators and absences matter before calling the positive labels reliable.

- **AR01 — Reaffirm and refine:** keep multi-function sets in [paragraph-functions.json](../../../docs/writing-harness/paragraph-functions.json). For a future frozen pilot, report exact sets, partial-set overlap, per-label positive agreement and raw marginals; test a suitable multi-valued alpha only after defining comparable units, missingness and its distance function. Two identical label sets can still target different claims; no agreement score alone makes them correct.
- **AR02 — Refine:** retain a justification quote, the complete function realization span, and the related claim/target as distinct objects, with `not_collected` or unresolved states where appropriate. Existing quotes in the local experiment are not necessarily full realizations. Offset consistency and target existence can be checked mechanically; semantic fit, span completeness and target scope require contextual review. For example, an illustration need not also support a claim.
- **AR03 — Refine:** preserve pre-adjudication independent records, coder backgrounds, training, sample selection, prevalence, unresolved/missing values, positive disagreements and target comparisons. Test independently selected boundaries separately from labels. Freeze any later decision criterion before evaluation; do not adopt the paper's 750-unit observation as a rule. High agreement may result from shared omissions, and reconciliation is not proof of truth.

## P14: Ten Simple (Empirical) Rules for Writing Science

Weinberger, Evans and Allesina (2015), [DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004205](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004205), is explicitly an **editorial containing an observational quantitative analysis**, not a systematic synthesis of interventions. Its ten headings summarize existing advice and the authors test lexical or structural proxies against citations. The complete six-page main text and five-page official supporting methods were read. The authors retrieved Scopus records indexed as Article in May 2013 for 1996–2012 across eight JCR categories. They excluded records with abstracts shorter than 50 words, no author or fewer than 10 references, leaving 1,065,139 category records (S1 pp. 1–2). Some journals appear in multiple categories, so this is not established as a count of unique articles; three fields are related biology areas, not independent replications. These filters also limit transfer to shorter abstracts and lightly referenced work. The outcome is citation count, a proxy for the authors' chosen success criterion—not reading comprehension, quality, author voice or evidence that a writing choice caused citations. The paper's proposed search-retrievability explanation is explicitly a hypothesis for future research (p. 4).

| Rule tested | Measured proxy | Reported citation association | What remains untested |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Short | Abstract word and sentence counts | Fewer of each associated with fewer citations | Whether extra content helps the intended reader or fits a real limit |
| 2. Compact | Sentence length relative to journal mean | Shorter sentences beneficial only in mathematics and physics | Clarity, syntax and connection between sentences |
| 3. Simple | Share of unique words in standard and easy-word dictionaries | Fewer common/easy words associated with more citations | Reader knowledge and appropriate technical vocabulary |
| 4. Present tense | (Present + gerund) / (present + gerund + past + past participle) | Positive in biology and psychology; negative in chemistry and physics | Accuracy of chronology and study status |
| 5. Few modifiers | Adjective/adverb share | More associated with more citations | Whether each modifier carries meaning or adds fluff |
| 6. Focus | Count of words matching keywords; unavailable if no keywords | Greater matching associated with fewer citations | Actual conceptual focus or usefulness of keywords |
| 7. Novelty/importance | Presence of marker words | Positive association | Whether novelty or importance is substantiated |
| 8. Boldness | Superlatives / (superlatives + comparatives) | Positive association | Accuracy, confidence calibration and reader trust |
| 9. Confidence | Fewer hedge words | Negative association in biology and physics, positive in chemistry | Whether qualifications accurately reflect uncertainty |
| 10. Avoid evocative words | Dictionary affect dimensions; unmatched words scored zero | Pleasant, active and imaginable word scores positively associated | Whether imagery, voice or meaning helps readers |

The proxies and directions are the authors' summary (main text pp. 1–3, Fig. 1); the source does not establish causal effects, and lexical proxies do not perfectly measure their headings. Their separate linear regressions use `log(citations + 1)` as the response, journal-year indicators and journal-standardized counts of authors, references and **one** abstract feature at a time; standardization uses all years in each journal (S1 pp. 2–3). Related writing features were not jointly controlled, and journal-year adjustment does not eliminate topic, research quality, visibility or other confounding. The exponentiated coefficient is exactly a model-scale ratio of predicted `citations + 1`; treating it as a citation percentage is an approximation, especially near zero (S1 p. 3).

Figure 1 uses `0.01/15` as a Bonferroni-adjusted criterion for 15 features (main text p. 3); the supplement says journal-level tests use an overall 0.05 threshold (S1 p. 3). The main text gives illustrative journal-relative one-standard-deviation associations of **+4.6%** for a mathematics novelty feature and **−7.2%** for a geology short-abstract feature (pp. 3–4, Fig. 2). Do not treat those approximate model-derived quantities as causal changes. The authors report sign changes in three of 15 features across their selected disciplines (p. 4), which does not mean effects have equal magnitude across fields. Feature-specific eligible counts, complete regression output and individual confidence intervals are not provided in the extracted supplement.

The official [supporting methods PDF](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article/file?type=supplementary&id=info:doi/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004205.s001) was subsequently retrieved through the publisher endpoint and read; the earlier web-tool attempt failed on a signed redirect. The [retrieval receipt](../supplement-attempts.json) records the PDF hash. S1 says keyword matching is unavailable when keywords are absent, but does not report the excluded count or the regression handling (p. 4). It labels the dictionary proxies R2a/R2b despite the main text using R3a/R3b (p. 3). The raw Scopus corpus and code have not been independently obtained or replicated.

- **AR04 — Reaffirm:** [editing guidance](../../../skills/edit-with-purpose/SKILL.md) already says a lower count alone does not establish easier reading; [accepted foundations](../../../docs/writing-harness/accepted-foundations.md) separate observations from actual targets and limits. Keep that distinction. A journal's actual word limit can require cutting even when longer abstracts correlate with citations.
- **AR05 — Reaffirm:** [drafting guidance](../../../skills/write-with-purpose/SKILL.md) protects precise terminology, voice and necessary qualification. Choose tense from chronology and study status, explain terminology for the actual reader, and retain modifiers that contribute meaning. A rare term may need definition for a novice; a dictionary frequency is not an audience test. Word and tense detection are mechanical; their appropriateness is an editorial judgment.
- **AR06 — Not justified:** do not add novelty claims, superlatives or remove hedges to improve citations. The [function catalogue](../../../docs/writing-harness/paragraph-functions.json) already distinguishes a hedge word from explaining a limitation and requires support to connect evidence to a claim. A cue can prompt evidence review, but cannot establish novelty or adequate qualification. An evidence-grounded result may accurately be called novel; the word itself does not demonstrate that fact.

## Reuse and unresolved questions

The [dataset-adoption comparison](../../dataset-adoption-2026-11-16/comparison.md) keeps section roles, passage functions, relations, actors and structural markers distinct; its proposed crosswalks are not paragraph gold. Existing adapters can check source IDs, offsets and links, but cannot decide whether a target truly supports a claim. Neither reviewed paper supplies a newly verified GitHub repository, raw annotation release, complete corpus license or installation-ready implementation. Appendix C of P18 is available in its paper, and the P14 supplementary methods have now been read; original coding matrices, P14 regression outputs and permitted raw source data remain retrieval targets; an implementation of multi-valued alpha would also need independently checked empty-set/missing semantics and licensing. No accepted skill, catalogue or prior packet was changed.
