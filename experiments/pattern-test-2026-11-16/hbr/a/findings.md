# HBR holdouts: two source-layout reviews

These are single-coder interpretations of two holdouts, not estimates of an HBR norm or independent agreement. The publisher page images were inspected for main-body reading order. Private reconstructions retain paragraph text and ordered block references; public coding records retain spans, hashes, labels, and page coordinates. The hypotheses below describe what the texts do, not whether their substantive claims are true.

**The Power of Talk.** The opening explicitly describes the author's sociolinguistic research and its workplace extension (unit 005). The article defines linguistic style (007), then develops labeled topics through explanations and examples: credit, confidence, questions, conversational rituals, authority and indirectness. The treatments vary; they are not a fixed succession of component definitions. The final paragraph (073) qualifies its advice by stating that no single solution fits every context, then urges managers to adapt to different linguistic styles. Two dialogue excerpts are retained as separate quotation units. The body ends in the right column on PDF p. 12; biography, pull quotes, page furniture and reprint notice are excluded. The page 3–4 join in unit 008 is supported by the flush-left continuation at the top of p. 4 and continuity of the turn-taking example; the top-of-page typography leaves modest residual uncertainty.

**Customers as Innovators.** The opening attributes the approach to the authors' cross-industry study (003), followed by examples from BBA, semiconductors and GE Plastics. The toolkit paragraph (013) develops four capabilities, but subsequent explicit sections trace industry change, benefits and challenges, value migration, and questions of value; they do not repeatedly define a standard set of components. The closing (040) proposes potentially broad effects while leaving the location and capture of value uncertain. Main prose runs from p. 5 through the right column of p. 11; the figures, p. 7 and p. 9 sidebars, p. 11 upper sidebar, authors' biography, notes and reprint material are excluded. Extraction omitted some spaces after punctuation and meaningful line-end hyphens. Known instances were repaired in a separate normalized copy with per-unit source hashes and edit records; residual normalization errors remain possible.

The shared engine produced exploratory full-body prose measures: Power has 7,302 word tokens in 71 prose paragraphs and 355 estimated sentences, with medians of 96 words per paragraph and 20 per sentence. Customers has 4,169 word tokens in 40 paragraphs and 196 estimated sentences, with medians of 99 and 21 words respectively. These are descriptions of these two reconstructions, not norms; source segmentation, normalization and automatic sentence splitting limit precision. Dialogue is counted separately from prose in Power. Section shares and detailed distributions are in `power-metrics-exploratory.json` and `customers-metrics-exploratory.json`. The earlier Power subset remains in `power-metrics-provisional.json` for audit.
