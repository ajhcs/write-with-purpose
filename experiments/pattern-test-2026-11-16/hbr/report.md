# HBR practitioner writing: exploratory pattern test

Four reconstructed HBR holdouts fit the primary **practitioner argument/perspective** comparison: *Managing Oneself*, *Using the Balanced Scorecard as a Strategic Management System*, *The Power of Talk*, and *Customers as Innovators*. Each advances a position about a professional problem, supported by explanations and examples. Their original dates range from 1995 to 2002. The 1998 development example *What Makes a Leader?* is excluded.

Two additional HBR articles, *Designing Services That Deliver* and *The House of Quality*, are coded **boundary cases**, because their dominant contribution is explanation of a procedure or technical design tool. They also advocate their methods, but their primary reader purpose differs from the argument cohort. A seventh retained article, *Manufacturing: The New Case for Vertical Integration*, has an inspected source but substantial OCR corruption; a preliminary form screen identifies a sustained practitioner argument for vertical integration, but its paragraph-level conclusions remain provisional pending full correction. A full-range OCR candidate has tentative codes; only two units were source-corrected. The initial pilot aim was six to ten eligible practitioner arguments; four are fully reviewed here, while six HBR texts have exploratory reconstructions. This is a bounded pilot, not a claim that the initial sample aim was met. Venue is not a substitute for form eligibility.

[documents.json](documents.json) indexes source paths and hashes, page/bounding-box traces, private text, sections, function labels, eligibility reasons and shared-engine measurements. The available-copy selection is not representative sampling. Each body received a single coder's multi-label interpretations from the twelve-function catalogue. Paragraphs may perform several functions; role counts and role-specific paragraph lengths overlap and must not be added into an overall budget. Figures, editorial summaries and non-body matter were excluded; lists and displayed dialogue have source-specific treatment. Normalization, paragraph joining and sentence splitting remain approximate. No causal claim about quality or universal norm follows.

## Primary-form pattern test

The hypothesis is an opening central claim, then research basis, then repeated **definition → example → explanation → application** for named components, then a qualified closing. Under a strict reading in which every component repeats that full sequence, **0/4** primary holdouts establish the entire ordered pattern. This is a single-coder interpretation, not proof that the pattern never works.

| Primary holdout | Main variation | Closing |
|---|---|---|
| Managing Oneself | Self-assessment questions, examples and advice; no dedicated early research-basis section. | Broad social-change claim rather than qualification of the recommendation. |
| Balanced Scorecard | Four named processes with cases and explanation; the opening reports experience with over 100 organizations. | Advocates the system; risks and uncertainty appear earlier. |
| Power of Talk | Author's sociolinguistic research appears early; varied communication topics use examples and explanation rather than a uniform component sequence. | Rejects a universal solution and calls for adaptation. |
| Customers as Innovators | Authors' cross-industry study appears early; toolkit capabilities lead into industry cases, challenges and value migration. | Leaves value generation and capture uncertain. |

For this pilot, a **central claim** is an identifiable practitioner proposition in the opening; an **early basis** is the author's own stated research or organizational work in the opening; **repeated complete component moves** require each named component to develop a definition, example, explanation and application in that order; and a **qualified ending** explicitly states a limitation, condition or unresolved outcome in the final passage. These are interpretive operational definitions, not requirements for good writing. The results for the four eligible, fully reviewed primary sources are:

| Test | Observed |
|---|---:|
| Opening central claim | 4/4 |
| Early stated author research or organizational work | 3/4 |
| Repeated complete ordered component moves | 0/4 |
| Qualified or unresolved ending | 2/4 |
| Entire proposed ordered sequence | 0/4 |

All four advance an opening proposal; three of four name their own research or organizational work early. The narrower research-basis tally does not mean the fourth lacks reasons, cases, or other evidence. Two of four end with an explicit qualification or unresolved condition. Definitions, examples, explanations and applications recur, but their order and granularity vary. Question-led self-management and process/framework argument are candidate variants, not adopted taxonomy or required templates.
