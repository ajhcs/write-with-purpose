# Concepts and claims

Use this reference when a definition, assertion state, or link to evidence changes the meaning of a piece. A reader should be able to recover what is claimed, about what, and within what bounds.

## Resolve the concept in context

Choose wording from the relevant project or disciplinary definition. For a disputed term, identify the definition and version, likely points of confusion, and the distinction the reader needs. Keep an unresolved mapping open if the sources use different terms for different concepts. Once the term is established, use plain language to explain its role.

## Preserve the kind of claim

In engineering, distinguish a requirement from its rationale, a proposed implementation, and evidence it meets the requirement. Keep design targets and measured results distinct. Follow adopted modal conventions and attach values to the correct quantity, unit, comparator, period, and operating condition. Retain threshold inclusivity, the range tested, and the assumptions that limit a model result. For a proposed change, name what would change and the intended consequence.

In clinical and research writing, preserve whose finding it is, who reported it, when it held, and whether it is present, absent, suspected, or unknown. Separate current status, prior history, and family history. An ordered, pending, or completed test has a different meaning at each stage. Treat a gap in documentation as unknown unless other evidence resolves it. For study results, keep the population, denominator, comparison, follow-up, and uncertainty that affect interpretation. An association supports only the degree of causal inference the study can justify. Equivalence needs its own evidence.

## Put claims and evidence together

Show the link from the evidence to the claim. Make it clear when a passage reports a fact, offers an interpretation, proposes a change, or uses an example. An observed actor, event, value, or outcome needs a source appropriate to that claim. A proposed remedy should address the defect the evidence identifies. Keep unsupported premises open even when the prose could be made to sound certain.

Select evidence for the reader's task. A summary may omit details while carrying the conditions needed to interpret the findings it keeps. A specified handoff, complete record, or transcription needs the coverage its brief sets.

## Review the final claims

Compare the actor, action, object, negation, modality, timing, and conditions in the final wording with the source. Check numbers with their roles and units attached. Use mechanical comparisons to locate changes worth reading in context.

Check fidelity to the source and factual correctness separately. Follow the authorized research scope. Report a conflict or unknown when it affects the result, and state what is supported.
