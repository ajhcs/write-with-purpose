description: Draft or substantially revise prose for clear meaning, useful structure and the author's voice. Use for requested writing or editorial work, including technical explanations, assignments and essays.
metadata:
  version: "0.3.2"
---

Help the person communicate what they intend. Their request controls purpose, facts, format and depth. Deliver the writing or requested editorial judgment; an existing passage can already be sufficient.

Work out the contribution before expanding the source material. Organize around the reader's question, the answer and its reasons, or the experience the piece develops. Select the evidence needed for that contribution and the context needed to interpret it. Honor actual coverage and length requirements. A short outline can help sustained work; a narrow edit may need no planning artifact.

Use familiar, exact words and stable concept names. Keep useful framing and technical terms that make real distinctions. Simplify a difficult connection by stating plainly what the terms refer to and how the ideas relate. Give important nouns a recoverable referent. Connect actors, actions, conditions and sequence through syntax; bind values to their entities, units, times and operating conditions. Preserve negation, attribution, diagnostic or evidential state, and the force of recommendations and requirements.

Make the relationship intelligible. Show how the relevant fact supports the conclusion. An instruction should say what changes and what happens before or after it; a broad label can leave the real instruction unstated. Put necessary qualifications in the claims they limit. Explain an inference the reader needs, while letting exact terms, tense, syntax and paired values carry distinctions they already express. Develop the paragraph through connected sentences; embed a brief definition or qualification where it can be read naturally with the idea it belongs to.

For substantial drafting, make a distinct reader edit before delivery. Read the passage as a standalone result and recover its answer, reasoning and next action from the words on the page. Repair an unclear link in plain clauses while retaining framing and precise terms that help the reader. Where two units do the same work, try a deletion or combination and compare the actual passages. Keep the version that carries the thought clearly and smoothly, with less unnecessary work for the reader. Include extra explanation or calculation when it resolves a real difficulty or the task asks for it. Useful repetition, rhythm and emotional effect can earn space. A purpose assigned after writing is weaker evidence than the unit's effect in context. Scale this edit to the task; a satisfactory narrow passage can stand.

Keep the author's voice. Use samples according to their assigned role: evidence, required content or expression reference. Select material for the present thought. When the preferred direction is unclear, offer a small concrete probe and keep the inference local to the person's reaction.

Use a reference to resolve a particular need:

| Need | Resource |
| --- | --- |
| A construction's scope, reference, punctuation or sequence needs examination | [Language and meaning](references/language-and-meaning.md) |
| A disputed definition or specific technical/clinical distinction needs checking | [Concepts and claims](references/concepts-and-claims.md) |
| An argument, paragraph progression or information-selection problem needs development | [Structure and purpose](references/structure-and-purpose.md) |
| A requested mechanical review or declared preservation obligation benefits from exact checks | [Advisory checks](references/checks.md) |

Load what resolves the issue. Check the selected claims against their sources separately from the reader edit: fidelity includes the context whose omission would mislead, rather than every available detail. Assess conformance, source fidelity/factual correctness and usefulness separately. A checker result or model approval establishes only what was actually assessed. Report material unresolved issues when the reader needs them. Keep editorial process notes separate unless requested. Stop when the task is served and another edit has no defensible benefit.
