Help the person communicate what they intend. Their explicit request takes priority over this guidance and controls purpose, facts, format and depth. A supplied draft is the starting text when the request is to clean it up; make local edits instead of generating a replacement, even if this writing skill was selected. Deliver the writing or requested editorial judgment; an existing passage can already be sufficient.

Work out the contribution before expanding the source material. Organize around the reader's question, the answer and its reasons, or the experience the piece develops. Select the evidence needed for that contribution and the context needed to interpret it. Honor actual coverage and length requirements. Establish the requested result and the role of each supplied passage: source evidence, wording to preserve, approved style reference or rejected output. Keep rejected output out of the style reference. Follow required terminology and the supplied format; use a brief or template only when it serves this task. A short outline can help sustained work; a narrow edit may need no planning artifact.

Keep the author's voice. Before drafting or revising to match a preferred sample, compare it with any output the author rejected. Follow the supported choices in wording, useful context, clause order, connecting phrases and paragraph flow. Avoid replacing those choices with a generic preference for fewer words, uniformly short sentences or active voice. Carry over how the thought develops without importing the sample's facts or changing the new claim's certainty. Keep precise technical terms. When a preference is unclear, show a short candidate and use the author's response to refine it; do not label a candidate accepted yourself.

Use familiar, exact words and stable concept names. Keep useful framing and technical terms that make real distinctions. Simplify a difficult connection by stating plainly what the terms refer to and how the ideas relate. Give important nouns a recoverable referent. Connect actors, actions, conditions and sequence through syntax; bind values to their entities, units, times and operating conditions. Preserve negation, attribution, diagnostic or evidential state, and the force of recommendations and requirements.

Make the relationship intelligible. Show how the relevant fact supports the conclusion. An instruction should say what changes and what happens before or after it; a broad label can leave the real instruction unstated. Put necessary qualifications in the claims they limit, even when that adds a word; honor any explicit length limit. Explain an inference the reader needs, while letting exact terms, tense, syntax and paired values carry distinctions they already express. Develop the paragraph through connected sentences; embed a brief definition or qualification where it can be read naturally with the idea it belongs to.

For substantial drafting, make a distinct reader edit before delivery. Read the passage as a standalone result and recover its answer, reasoning and next action from the words on the page. Repair an unclear link in plain clauses while retaining framing and precise terms that help the reader. Compare what neighboring sentences add, including paraphrases that repeat a claim without repeating its words. Where two units do the same work, try a deletion or combination and compare the actual passages. Retain examples and necessary conditions; remove a second explanation when it supplies neither. For lists of illustrative examples, select what establishes the point; keep the full set only when coverage requires it. State the supported actor, action and consequence when a phrase hides the relationship. A request for plain language permits replacing figurative wording; preserve necessary conditions and technical meanings. Avoid a new metaphor or abstract label that merely restates the idea. Keep the version that carries the thought clearly and smoothly, with less unnecessary work for the reader. Include extra explanation or calculation when it resolves a real difficulty or the task asks for it. Useful repetition, rhythm and emotional effect can earn space. A purpose assigned after writing is weaker evidence than the unit's effect in context. Scale this edit to the task; a satisfactory narrow passage can stand.

Verify an explicit word ceiling or no-growth requirement against the final delivered text with an available counter. The bundled checker can enforce these declared constraints; see [Advisory checks](references/checks.md). A failed limit calls for another scoped edit, or a clear explanation of an unresolved conflict, not a silent relaxation or a claim of compliance. With no counting tool, state the verification limit when exact compliance matters.

Load what resolves the issue. Check the selected claims against their sources separately from the reader edit: fidelity includes the context whose omission would mislead, rather than every available detail. Assess conformance, source fidelity/factual correctness and usefulness separately. A checker result or model approval establishes only what was actually assessed. Report material unresolved issues when the reader needs them. Keep editorial process notes separate unless requested. Stop when the task is served and another edit has no defensible benefit.
