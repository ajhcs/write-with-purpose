# Structure, development and improvement

Use this reference when the author wants to develop an idea, reconsider an argument or arrangement, or learn from a writing choice. Work at the depth the brief supports. Fix a named error locally. For polish, improve the specified quality while preserving choices that work. For broader improvement, consider the reasoning, arrangement, and expression even if individual sentences are sound.

## Develop a contribution

Find what the author observed, thinks, proposes, or is still trying to decide. Build on their actual reasons. Supply a connection the available evidence supports, or mark a new interpretation as a possibility for the author to consider. When a premise is missing, complete the parts that can stand and surface the decision it affects.

Test an important claim against the objection, competing explanation, or boundary most likely to change it. For example, a growing backlog alone supports investigating capacity. A recommendation to hire two people also needs evidence about how much capacity the work requires and what the team can already handle. If the brief opens the recommendation to revision, develop the strongest decision the available facts support.

## Choose a form for the reader's task

Use **Family → Form → Section** when the structure is itself part of the work. Family groups shared conventions, form gives the reader a more specific expectation, and section describes a part's purpose. A part can work without a heading, and a paragraph can do several jobs. Use the requested template for its required parts and adapt the rest to the piece.

A decision memo can lead with a recommendation and the conditions for acting on it. A reflection can let the reader discover a question alongside the author. A tutorial needs the prerequisites and an observable result. When helping an author plan, prompts such as “What would change your choice?” can reveal the material a particular section needs.

For a requested form analysis, describe document length, section structure and proportions, paragraph and sentence shape, word length, examples and evidence, presentation, and communicative functions. Record an observed range, a working target, and a declared limit under their actual source, scope, and unit. Use each for its own purpose. A corpus median describes the corpus.

## Compare the consequential change

Write the transition or representative passage that makes the choice concrete. Put it back in context to judge the effect. A different order can change when the reader encounters a necessary qualification, what claim they take as the author's position, or how a story unfolds. Carry the chosen arrangement through the rest of the piece and reread the affected connections. Write a second complete version when the brief requests one or the consequence depends on the whole.

Cole Lyons singled out the first of these lines in a comparison of his CS50 essay.

> The speed makes it all the more useful to know what each part is meant to do and to check what happens when the parts meet.

> I still need to know what each part is supposed to do and check what happens when the parts meet.

Both lines preserve the duty to check. The first also tells the reader why faster work makes the check more valuable. Compare what the reader can explain after each version, including what the original did well. Use rhythm, an image, repetition, or delay when its effect serves the piece. A smaller edit and a shorter sentence are possible means. Judge the actual effect on the reader, and leave it open if the comparison cannot settle it.

## Finish the writing and teach only as wanted

Read the result as the intended reader will encounter it. Check that the argument, instruction, or experience carries through, and verify material claims against the evidence. For an export, compare the supplied visual reference at the intended size and try any links or controls central to the reader's task. Correct issues against the brief and review the affected result again. Keep any delivery notes distinct from the usable text when the boundary matters, including for counts. Editorial judgment is provisional until author feedback or reader evidence is available.

When the author asks to learn, explain a specific choice using the actual wording and its effect. Deliver a requested draft or edit and follow the author's sequence for any practice. For attempt-first practice, give facts that leave the writing choice open. For example, “correct classifications: 80; evaluated cases: 100; comparator: none supplied” leaves the sentence to compose. “The model correctly classified 80 of 100 cases” supplies it. Let the author try before showing the answer. Give a worked solution directly when asked.
