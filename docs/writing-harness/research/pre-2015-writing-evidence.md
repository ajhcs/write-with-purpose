# Pre-2015 evidence for a writing harness

Initial scoping search, 15 November 2026. Preferred publication cutoff: 31 December 2014. This is a source register and research starting point, not an exhaustive systematic review, a new meta-analysis, or a completed critical appraisal. Publication years below come from article or publisher metadata, not search-engine crawl dates.

The [16 November expansion](genre-synthesis-and-corpus-plan.md) investigates genre/category syntheses beyond instruction, adds cross-domain research, and records substantial acquired discovery inventories plus a staged corpus plan.

## What the search found

There are pre-2015 quantitative meta-analyses of **writing instruction**. They evaluate teaching interventions, usually with school students. For our question about recurring functions and structures in successful writing, **genre analysis, rhetorical move analysis, and corpus-based discourse analysis** are closer matches. A survey of style manuals provides a third evidence type: what guidance recommends, which may differ from what authors actually do.

This search did not establish a single meta-analysis that supplies validated paragraph-function categories and numerical ranges across personal essays, technical documentation, and academic papers. That is a search outcome, not evidence that no such synthesis exists. Most directly relevant findings located here concern academic or student writing; professional web writing and manuals remain undercovered.

## Source register

The summaries below stay within the material actually inspected. Publisher previews indexed by search were used when direct access failed; a preview is not a full-paper review. No effect sizes have been pooled or converted into writing rules.

### E01 — Graham and Perin, 2007

**A Meta-Analysis of Writing Instruction for Adolescent Students.** *Journal of Educational Psychology*, 99(3), 445–476. DOI: [10.1037/0022-0663.99.3.445](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.99.3.445). [Author-supplied ERIC abstract](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ772018).

- **Evidence:** quantitative synthesis of experimental and quasi-experimental writing interventions in Grades 4–12; 123 documents yielding 154 effect sizes.
- **Finding relevant here:** the abstract reports beneficial average effects for strategy instruction, studying models, setting product goals, and several other interventions.
- **Design implication, our inference:** make the writing strategy, example's function, and intended outcome visible. Test whether this helps the author learn, beyond producing a draft.
- **Limit / access:** author abstract inspected through the search index; full methods not appraised. School-based interventions do not establish efficacy for adult experts or LLM scaffolds. Traditional grammar instruction in this analysis is not equivalent to a modern diagnostic linter.

### E02 — Graham, McKeown, Kiuhara, and Harris, 2012

**A Meta-Analysis of Writing Instruction for Students in the Elementary Grades.** *Journal of Educational Psychology*, 104(4), 879–896. DOI: [10.1037/a0029185](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0029185). [Author-supplied ERIC abstract](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ994038); [publisher supplement and DOI confirmation](https://supp.apa.org/psycarticles/supplemental/a0029185/a0029185_supp.html).

- **Evidence:** quantitative synthesis using 115 documents; intervention categories required at least four studies.
- **Finding relevant here:** text-structure instruction, strategies, prewriting, goals, and assessment were among the interventions with positive reported effects on writing quality.
- **Design implication, our inference:** test contribution-based scaffolds and focused revision feedback as teaching supports.
- **Limit / access:** author abstract inspected through indexed results; full methods not appraised. The population is elementary students. The result does not identify an ideal paragraph length or validate our twelve functions.

### E03 — Graham and Sandmel, 2011

**The Process Writing Approach: A Meta-analysis.** *Journal of Educational Research*, 104(6), 396–407. DOI: [10.1080/00220671.2010.488703](https://doi.org/10.1080/00220671.2010.488703). [Author institutional record and abstract](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/the-process-writing-approach-a-meta-analysis/).

- **Evidence:** 29 experimental and quasi-experimental studies, Grades 1–12.
- **Finding relevant here:** a modest average improvement in general-education writing quality; no statistically significant motivation improvement or improvement in struggling writers' composition quality in the reported analysis.
- **Design implication, our inference:** assess particular supports and populations. More workflow stages alone are not an established solution.
- **Limit / access:** institutional abstract inspected; full methods not appraised. This does not test an AI-assisted writing process.

### E04 — Bennett, 2009

**English Academic Style Manuals: A Survey.** *Journal of English for Academic Purposes*, 8(1), 43–54. DOI: [10.1016/j.jeap.2008.12.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeap.2008.12.003). [Publisher preview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1475158508001069).

- **Evidence:** survey of prescriptive style manuals conducted during 2004–2007, not an intervention meta-analysis or a corpus of published papers.
- **Finding relevant here:** substantial agreement in the manuals about principles and textual construction, alongside differences.
- **Design implication, our inference:** shared conventions are a plausible seed for the catalogue. Compare those prescriptions with actual prose before enforcing them.
- **Limit / access:** abstract, introduction, and indexed section snippets inspected. The author's initial checklist reflected expectations; this is a reason to use counterexamples and independent annotation in our own work. Conflicting sample-size claims in later citations were not resolved, so no manual count is asserted here.

### E05 — Kanoksilapatham, 2005

**Rhetorical Structure of Biochemistry Research Articles.** *English for Specific Purposes*, 24(3), 269–292. DOI: [10.1016/j.esp.2004.08.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esp.2004.08.003). [Publisher preview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0889490604000705).

- **Evidence:** move analysis of 60 biochemistry research articles, with a compiled discipline-specific corpus and a coding-reliability procedure.
- **Finding relevant here:** proposes 15 moves across Introduction, Methods, Results, and Discussion, with a second level of steps.
- **Design implication, our inference:** use form/section-specific moves, explicit definitions, and a second annotation of boundaries. This is a particularly close methodological starting point.
- **Limit / access:** publisher abstract and introduction inspected through indexed preview; direct open returned 403. The full coding protocol and results still need extraction. These moves are not our paragraph functions one-for-one, and the corpus does not represent all science writing.

### E06 — Yang and Allison, 2003

**Research Articles in Applied Linguistics: Moving from Results to Conclusions.** *English for Specific Purposes*, 22(4), 365–385. DOI: [10.1016/S0889-4906(02)00026-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0889-4906(02)00026-1). [Publisher preview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0889490602000261).

- **Evidence:** genre analysis of 20 empirical articles in applied linguistics.
- **Finding relevant here:** examines alternative Results, combined Results and Discussion, Discussion, Conclusion, and Pedagogic Implications arrangements; proposes moves and steps within Discussion sections.
- **Design implication, our inference:** section labels and functions are not interchangeable. Represent alternative section arrangements and links between neighboring sections.
- **Limit / access:** indexed publisher abstract and introduction inspected; direct open returned 403. Small disciplinary corpus, not a universal sequence or a causal test of writing quality.

### E07 — Biber, Connor, and Upton, 2007

**Discourse on the Move: Using Corpus Analysis to Describe Discourse Structure.** John Benjamins, Studies in Corpus Linguistics 28. DOI: [10.1075/scl.28](https://doi.org/10.1075/scl.28). [Publisher description and contents](https://benjamins.com/catalog/scl.28).

- **Evidence:** methodological research volume illustrated by genre-specific case studies, not a quantitative meta-analysis.
- **Contribution relevant here:** combines discourse analysis of individual texts with corpus-level description. Contrasts starting with functional categories against deriving textual categories from linguistic patterns.
- **Design implication, our inference:** seed labels from guidelines, then let corpus examples revise the categories. Avoid measuring only the distinctions we expected to find.
- **Limit / access:** publisher description and contents inspected, not the full volume. The book is a research source; books remain outside our initial target writing forms.

### E08 — Biber and Gray, 2010

**Challenging Stereotypes about Academic Writing: Complexity, Elaboration, Explicitness.** *Journal of English for Academic Purposes*, 9(1), 2–20. DOI: [10.1016/j.jeap.2010.01.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeap.2010.01.001). [Author-hosted article](https://jan.ucc.nau.edu/biber/Biber/Biber_Gray_2010.pdf).

- **Evidence:** comparative corpus research on grammatical features of academic writing and conversation.
- **Finding relevant here:** academic prose often concentrates information in noun phrases and leaves relationships implicit, rather than simply using more subordinate clauses. The authors discuss the challenge this presents to novice readers.
- **Design implication, our inference:** measure sentence length as one descriptive signal. Inspect conceptual density, assumed vocabulary, and implicit relationships separately when assessing audience fit.
- **Limit / access:** full PDF available; abstract and opening argument inspected. This search did not independently reproduce the corpus analysis or assess all methods. No grade-level threshold follows from the paper.

### E09 — McNamara, Crossley, and McCarthy, 2010

**Linguistic Features of Writing Quality.** *Written Communication*, 27(1), 57–86. DOI: [10.1177/0741088309351547](https://doi.org/10.1177/0741088309351547). [Author institutional record and abstract](https://experts.azregents.edu/en/publications/linguistic-features-of-writing-quality/).

- **Evidence:** computational analysis of expert-rated student essays using Coh-Metrix indices.
- **Finding relevant here:** syntactic complexity, lexical diversity, and word frequency predicted ratings in this dataset; the tested cohesion indices did not distinguish the high- and low-rated groups. Higher ratings aligned with some features of linguistic difficulty.
- **Design implication, our inference:** a rubric's quality rating, ease of comprehension, and author voice are different outcomes. Do not optimize a draft toward one readability or sophistication score.
- **Limit / access:** institutional abstract inspected; full methods not appraised. These are associations in a particular essay corpus, not instructions to increase complexity.

### E10 — Mann and Thompson, 1988

**Rhetorical Structure Theory: Toward a Functional Theory of Text Organization.** *Text*, 8(3), 243–281. [Original paper hosted by the RST project](https://www.sfu.ca/rst/05bibliographies/bibs/Mann_Thompson_1988.pdf); [project bibliography confirming publication](https://www.sfu.ca/rst/05bibliographies/reports.html).

- **Evidence:** foundational descriptive theory of relations between spans of text, not a meta-analysis or a writing-quality intervention.
- **Contribution relevant here:** gives explicit ways to describe how parts of a text contribute to its organization through relations.
- **Design implication, our inference:** store a passage's function separately from the passage it supports, qualifies, contrasts with, or elaborates.
- **Limit / access:** original abstract/opening and project introduction inspected; full relation formalism not implemented. Annotation remains interpretive and may admit alternatives.

## Search record and unresolved work

Discovery used web searches, with primary publisher, author-hosted, institutional, and author-supplied ERIC records preferred for evidence. Representative queries actually run:

```text
"A meta-analysis of writing instruction for adolescent students" Graham Perin 2007
"A meta-analysis of writing instruction for students in elementary grades" 2012
"The process writing approach" "meta-analysis" Graham Sandmel 2011
"meta-analysis" "writing quality" "text structure" before:2015-01-01
"writing" "meta-analysis" "rhetorical structure" before:2015-01-01
"English academic style manuals" "survey" Bennett 2009
"Rhetorical structure of biochemistry research articles" 2005
"Research articles in applied linguistics" "moving from results to conclusions" 2003
"Discourse on the Move" Biber Connor Upton 2007 moves corpus
"Challenging stereotypes about academic writing" Biber Gray 2010
"Linguistic features of writing quality" McNamara Crossley McCarthy 2010
```

Follow-up searches restricted several queries to ERIC, institutional sites, and publishers to verify claims. Later publications surfaced during discovery but were not included as pre-2015 evidence. Current Harvard, Purdue, and Manchester guidance is useful background; current webpages are not being assigned historical publication dates.

Next work should retrieve full methods for E01–E06 and E09, extract E05's move definitions and reliability procedure, and compare E06's alternative section arrangements. Broaden discovery toward professional instructions, personal essays, and web genres. Record each study's population, unit of analysis, selection method, genre, discipline, language, annotation reliability, outcomes, and available distributions before pooling anything.

For our own corpus, select worthwhile examples before measuring the desired features; preserve reasons for selection, era, author, outlet, intended reader, and form variant. Include contrasting successful patterns. Annotate a pilot with independent readers, recording span-boundary and label disagreements separately; use disagreements to revise the guide. Hold out authors/outlets when evaluating whether the learned patterns generalize.

Numerical ranges must come from comparable measured texts with a documented parser and sampling frame. Curated high-quality examples can describe that collection; they do not estimate all writing in the form. Citation count, reach, and prizes can identify candidates but are not independent proof that a structure caused success. No numerical profiles or universal minimum/maximum limits have been inferred from this search.
